Decision support

https://tarimportali.net/yapay-zeka-dijital-tarim/teknoloji/karar

A decision support system turns measurement into a recommendation: how much nitrogen to apply at which point, when to irrigate, where a fire will spread. The idea is older than machine learning: COMAX, the expert system the US Department of Agriculture developed for cotton, re-evaluated its recommendations every day using weather conditions reported by sensors in the field, and in 1988 close to 150 farmers used the model. Today machine learning is a candidate for deriving the recommendation directly from data, but two findings cut across fields: a model that predicts yield well may not find the right rate, and a recommendation that pays may still not be adopted. In Türkiye, an AI-supported decision support system has been used against forest fires since 2022, but no independent assessment of its effect has been published; the ministry's fertiliser recommendation method missed the phosphorus differences within the land where it was tested.

Good predictions, different rates

The hard part of variable-rate application is not the machine but the prescription. De Lara and colleagues (2023) fed the data of 20 on-farm trials in wheat and barley, with soil, terrain and satellite variables, to two machine learning methods. The models predicted yield with an average error of 13.7%; yet two models predicting with similar accuracy produced very different economically optimal nitrogen rates. The size and direction of the influential variables changed from field to field; according to the authors, a model trained on data from other fields may recommend unreliable rates in a new field.

In irrigation, AI is promising in simulation but little tested in the field. Saikai, Peake and Chenu (2023) used deep reinforcement learning to develop a rule that looks each day at soil water, the crop's growth stage and rainfall and decides how much to irrigate. Compared with the rule common in the region, it raised profit in every test year of 2011-2020, most of all in 2018, by 17%. The result comes from a crop-model simulation of Australia's Goondiwindi region, not from a field trial; the authors themselves note that research on the technique remains sparse and impractical.

Even a recommendation that pays can be dropped

In Mexico's Yaqui Valley, CIMMYT and Oklahoma State University developed GreenSeeker, a cheap handheld sensor that looks at the crop mid-season and gives a field-specific nitrogen recommendation. According to a 2017 RTI report, since the pilot phase in 2006 the sensor had been used on more than 48,000 hectares and had brought farmers 37 dollars of extra profit per hectare, not counting the cost of the device and the technician. According to the same team's 2022 peer-reviewed study, farmers who did not follow the recommendations made lower profits. Even so, adoption fell rather than expanded in the 2015-2016 season: the farmer credit unions, which also sell fertiliser, were reluctant to approve a change that meant using less of it, and funding for the technicians' sensor-based advice was shrinking. The report's conclusion: profit and environmental benefit were not enough to sustain adoption.

Advice from the state: fires, fertiliser, irrigation

According to a 2024 ministry news item, the General Directorate of Forestry has since 2022 used a Decision Support System that combines meteorological data, forest information system (ORBİS) data, and drone and satellite images; it estimates the likelihood and cause of fires and, with AI support, predicts a fire's behaviour and spread. Its effect on detection time, burned area or false alarm rate has not been independently measured and published.

In the field, the state's own recommendation was put to the test: on 300 hectares in Çankırı, where phosphorus varied moderately (coefficient of variation 21.3%), the ministry's fertiliser recommendation method gave exactly the same phosphorus rate to all five equal-sized sub-regions (Wardamı, Erşahin and Karahan, 2024). In irrigation, decision support is still being set up: according to the 2027 Programme, the Digital Irrigation Management System (DİSU) is to be completed, and work will continue on the use of decision-support systems in irrigation management and on the regulations to support it.
