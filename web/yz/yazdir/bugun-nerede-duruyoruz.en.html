TARIM PORTALI · FRAMEWORK

Where things stand today

AI in agriculture delivers its most solid results in work that keeps constant watch over a large area or a large number of objects: weather forecasting, mapping from satellites and fishing surveillance, with promising early results in detecting forest fires. When the work comes down to decisions on a single field, the picture becomes mixed: savings are measured in most trials, but profit does not show up on every field, and models that perform well in the lab fall short in the field. The technology is used most by large farms: in the EU, farms that apply precision farming make up roughly 18% of all farms yet manage about 44% of the farmland. In Türkiye the ministry has begun to use AI in monitoring work, but there is no published evaluation measuring the effect of AI on yields, input use or farmers' income in the field.

Watching over large areas: the most solid results

AI has moved from research to operations in weather forecasting. GraphCast outperformed the most accurate operational forecasting systems on 90% of 1,380 verification targets in 10-day global forecasts; the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts has been running its own AI model alongside its physics-based model since 25 February 2025. A map built from satellite imagery and deep learning revealed that 72-76% of the world's industrial fishing vessels are not publicly tracked. According to its preliminary results published in 2020, a camera-based prototype system usually detected the smoke of a forest fire within 15 minutes of ignition, with less than one false alarm per camera per day on average.

Decisions on the field: savings are measured, profit is inconsistent

Systems that see weeds with a camera and spray only on the weeds cut herbicide use by 35% in sugarcane and by 47% in early applications in maize in peer-reviewed field trials. Sensor-based nitrogen management raises nitrogen use efficiency in winter wheat by about 10.4%; but in a field study in dryland maize in Nebraska, a profit advantage appeared on only 35% of the fields. In irrigation the most solid evidence comes not from AI but from soil moisture sensors: an average water saving of 38% compared with traditional methods, but only 1% compared with methods based on canopy temperature. When machine learning was tested against the European Commission's operational yield forecasting system, which does not use machine learning, it matched the system for some crops and made twice the error for others; deep learning, for its part, showed no clear superiority over conventional models in genomic prediction.

The distance between the lab and the field

A plant disease model trained on images taken in controlled conditions was 99.35% accurate on images held out from the same set, but fell to just above 31% on images taken in other conditions. The 50 harvesting robots in a review published in 2014 had an average harvest success of 66%, and none had been commercialised. On 32 real questions from farmers in Nigeria, ChatGPT's answers scored higher than those of extension officers, but fell behind on site-specific matters such as planting time and seed and fertiliser rates. Three agricultural technology companies that presented AI-based systems, Infarm, AppHarvest and Small Robot Company, went into administration, bankruptcy protection or liquidation in 2023-2024.

Who uses it

In the US, 22% of farms reported using some precision agriculture practice in 2025, while, according to a 2023 report by the US Department of Agriculture, automated guidance was used on more than half of the planted acreage of six major crops. According to a global analysis, only 24-37% of farms smaller than 1 hectare are covered by 3G or 4G, against 74-80% of those larger than 200 hectares. Of the 27 digital automation service providers FAO examined, only 10 were profitable, and they mostly served large-scale producers.

Türkiye

The Türkiye Artificial Intelligence Action Plan (2026-2030) counts agriculture among four priority sectors, but the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry is neither the responsible body nor a partner in any of the plan's 16 actions. The ministry's own units, however, use AI, or are preparing to, in monitoring work:

  • Crop identification from satellites was applied for the first time in the 2025 production year.
  • An AI-assisted decision support system for forest fires has been in use since 2022.
  • The ministry's research arm TAGEM started three AI projects in 2025.

What these technologies meet is small farms and an ageing farming population: the average farm in the Farmer Registration System is 6.9 hectares, and the average age of people working in agriculture is 46. By the end of 2025, 12.91% of the loan for a World Bank-financed climate-smart agriculture project had been spent, and grant spending was zero. As for spraying drones, according to a survey of 71 operators, only 14.1% of operators calibrate before every application.
