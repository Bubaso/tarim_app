TARIM PORTALI · TECHNOLOGY

Predictive models

https://tarimportali.net/yapay-zeka-dijital-tarim/teknoloji/tahmin

In agriculture, predictive models try to anticipate the weather, yields, prices and an animal's genetic merit. AI delivers its most solid forecasting results in weather prediction, where it has moved from research into operations. In yields, prices and breeding, the advantage of machine learning over classical methods depends on the crop, the country and the data. In almost every field, what is measured is forecast accuracy; how much a better forecast changes losses or gains on the farm is mostly not measured. According to the ministry's description, Türkiye's official harvest estimate uses no model, and AI-based drought forecasting is planned to begin in 2027.

Weather forecasting: from research to operations

GraphCast, a machine learning model that learns from past weather data (Lam and colleagues, 2023), does markedly better than the most accurate operational systems on 90% of 1,380 verification targets in 10-day global forecasts. Since 25 February 2025 the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts has run its own AI system alongside its physics-based model; according to the centre, the system outperforms physics-based models on many measures, with gains of up to 20% on measures such as tropical cyclone tracks.

These studies measure accuracy; they do not measure how much a better frost or drought forecast reduces a farmer's losses. One measurement of a forecast turning into a decision comes from India: probabilistic forecasts of local monsoon onset were sent by text message to more than 38.8 million farmers in 13 states, and according to follow-up surveys in two states, 31-52% of farmers changed their sowing decisions based on them.

Racing classical methods: the gain varies

Even in an established system, accuracy varies by country: in the European Commission's MARS yield forecasting system, the median error for 2006-2015 was 3.73% in France and 14.37% in Portugal (Van der Velde and Nisini, 2019). A machine learning model built on the same system's data matched it for some crops and made twice its error for others (Paudel and colleagues, 2021).

In prices, AI brings a measurable improvement. On daily wholesale prices in India, the GRU deep learning model beat the classical ARIMA model by a wide margin: its error (RMSE) for onions was 370 against 1,565 (Manogna and colleagues, 2025). Even so, its mean percentage error was 10.58% for tomatoes and 14.59% for onions; at an Italian food wholesaler, ARIMA and LSTM networks performed similarly (Menculini and colleagues, 2021). In breeding, deep learning showed no clear superiority over conventional models in genomic prediction (Montesinos-López and colleagues, 2021).

A sound forecast is not always a sound decision

In the wheat and barley trials of De Lara and colleagues (2023), machine learning predicted yield with an average error of 13.7%, yet two models of similar accuracy produced very different economically optimal nitrogen rates. One prediction whose effect has been measured is genomic selection: in US Holsteins it cut the generation interval of sires of bulls from about 7 years to under 2.5 years and raised the annual genetic gain in yield traits by 50-100% (García-Ruiz and colleagues, 2016).

From harvest bulletins to drought forecasts

According to the ministry, official crop production estimates in Türkiye rest on district directorates' records and agricultural technicians' impressions, with neither satellites nor models. FAO-supported agro-meteorological yield forecast bulletins for winter wheat began in the 2005-2006 season, but recent bulletins give no figure per decare. In 2025 the ministry began using machine learning to identify crops from satellites; whether this feeds into harvest estimates has not been disclosed. There is no published assessment in Türkiye, either, comparing an AI-based harvest forecast with official estimates or actual production.

The General Directorate of Meteorology's Agricultural Frost Warning System provides 5-day frost forecasts by crop for every province and district. The directorate says it has used AI-supported forecasting systems since 2025, but has not disclosed which forecasts use them or how accurate they are. The only concrete AI task in the agriculture part of the 2027 Annual Programme is a forecast too: work on forecasting drought 1-4 weeks ahead with the Agricultural Drought Monitoring System is planned to begin in 2027.
