TARIM PORTALI · TECHNOLOGY

Language models

https://tarimportali.net/yapay-zeka-dijital-tarim/teknoloji/dil

Language models entered agriculture through advisory work. After ChatGPT was opened to the public on 30 November 2022, AI chatbots began answering farmers' questions; in India, one of them has answered millions. In the most direct comparison, in Nigeria, ChatGPT's answers generally scored higher than those of extension agents, but fell behind them on site- and time-specific matters such as when to sow and how much seed and fertiliser to apply. The comparisons made for language models measure answer quality; they do not measure the effect of language-model advice on farmers' yields or incomes. In Türkiye, language models have begun to be tested in academia with multiple-choice farm questions; the ministry has not yet announced a farmer-facing AI assistant or chatbot.

Success counted in questions

India's voice-enabled AI chatbot Kisan e-Mitra began answering questions about government schemes in 2023; according to a government note of February 2026, it had answered more than 9.3 million questions by December 2025, and it handles more than 8,000 questions a day in 11 regional languages. According to a non-peer-reviewed paper by its developers, Digital Green's FarmerChat, a chatbot powered by generative AI, reached more than 15,000 farmers in four countries. These figures show the scale of use; they do not show whether the answers were right or how they shaped farmers' decisions.

Fluent but not local

The effect of digital advice had been measured before language models arrived: according to meta-analyses, farm information delivered by mobile phone and digital channels raises yields by 4% and the odds of adopting recommended inputs by 22% (Fabregas, Kremer and Schilbach, 2019). In Nigeria, a mobile app giving farmer-specific advice on fertilising rice raised yields by 7% and profits by 10%; according to the authors, blanket fertiliser advice had failed to achieve potential yield gains in much of Sub-Saharan Africa (Arouna and colleagues, 2021).

This is exactly where language models struggle. Ibrahim and colleagues (2024) had 32 questions on irrigated rice, collected from farmers in Kano State, Nigeria, answered by six extension agents and by ChatGPT; four evaluators scored the answers for quality and local relevance. ChatGPT received the best score nearly six times as often as the agents (40% against 7%), and the evaluators preferred it in 78% of cases. But it fell behind on planting time, seed rate, and fertiliser rate and timing. According to the authors, building site-specific input rates and timing into AI assistants is critical if farmers are to use them directly.

In Türkiye, accuracy is tested, impact is not

A study from Karamanoğlu Mehmetbey University (Eldem and Eldem, 2026) found that on a set of 90 four-option farm questions, GPT-4o's accuracy ranged from 84.8% to 95.3% depending on the prompting strategy, and Gemini's from 74.8% to 88.4%; the lowest results came when the models were asked without examples. The questions were compiled from three online question banks; no published study has tested language models with Turkish, open-ended, site-specific farmer questions. A study in 96 tomato fields in Karacabey, Bursa (Şahin and colleagues, 2025) connected ChatGPT-4 to a model that recognises pests in images; the success measured belongs to the detection model (98.91% precision), and there is no measurement of the accuracy of the explanations and recommendations given by the language model.

The ministry has 17 mobile apps and says it is working on machine learning, deep learning and natural language processing, but it has not announced a farmer-facing AI assistant or chatbot. In the 2027 Programme, the only measure for a chatbot running on AI is for the tax administration; the Sectoral Turkish Large Language Model Call, under the responsibility of the Ministry of Industry and Technology, is to encourage adapting domestic language models to sectors. The effect of AI or language-model advice on farmers' yields, incomes or input use in Türkiye has not yet been measured and published.
