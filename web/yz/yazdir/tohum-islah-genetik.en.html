TARIM PORTALI · THE FIELD'S CALENDAR

Seed, breeding and genetics

https://tarimportali.net/yapay-zeka-dijital-tarim/tohum-islah-genetik

In the conventional system of the Türkiye-based International Winter Wheat Improvement Program, 9-10 years pass from cross to international distribution. The digital tools of breeding try to shorten that time and make selection more accurate: genomic selection can prove more accurate in early generations than selection based on field measurements, and speed breeding fits several generations into a year. The programme, too, has turned to speed breeding and molecular markers. AI, meanwhile, is a promising tool in breeding that has not yet replaced classical statistics; for a programme with little data, its advantage is uncertain.

What it does

Breeding means sifting a few lines out of thousands of crosses through years of trials. The most expensive and slowest link is testing: to see a line's yield and its tolerance of drought and disease, it has to be sown in different places and years. Deciding which lines enter these expensive trials is one of the critical choices of any breeding programme.

Digital tools enter this bottleneck at three points. In genomic selection, a model is built from a population of plants whose DNA has been read and whose performance has been measured in the field; the model then predicts the performance of new lines from their DNA markers. In phenotyping, plant traits are measured with sensors and images, so that more lines can be followed at once than could ever be measured by hand. Speed breeding controls light and temperature to grow more generations in a year.

In this chain, AI is being tested in particular in the genomic prediction model: deep learning in place of classical statistical models.

What the field says

Michel and colleagues (2016) compared early-generation selection on several years of data from a winter wheat line breeding programme. Prediction based on field measurements alone was not very accurate for grain yield (correlation 0.21); genomic selection models were more accurate across years (0.39). Genomic assisted selection, which combines the two, raised prediction accuracy by 20%.

Watson and colleagues (2018) count long generation times among the reasons breeding progresses slowly, and show that speed breeding can reach up to 6 generations a year in spring wheat, durum wheat, barley, chickpea and pea, and 4 in canola, against 2-3 in a normal glasshouse. The authors expect that combining the method with tools such as genomic selection could speed breeding up further.

Where AI stands

Montesinos-López and colleagues (2021) reviewed the applications of deep learning in genomic selection. The conclusion is measured: in the plant and animal breeding literature, deep learning showed no clear superiority in predictive power over conventional genomic models. It has strengths: it captures non-linear patterns better and can integrate data from different sources. But its first requirement is high-quality and sufficiently large training data; the authors stress that it should be applied to large data sets.

Türkiye

The 2027 Programme counts plant breeding and the conservation of genetic resources as strategic for climate adaptation and productivity. Among the work that must continue, it lists:

  • The conservation of local varieties and genetic resources
  • The development of varieties tolerant of drought, heat, disease and pests
  • Greater capacity to use biotechnology and modern breeding methods

The programme's agricultural measures, however, contain no item dedicated to genomic selection or to AI in breeding; in the agriculture chapter, the only genomics item is in livestock: an additional support coefficient for breeding heifers that have been genomically tested. The R&D chapter lists genome editing, together with AI, among the technology areas for which R&D infrastructure is to be built, but does not link it to agriculture.

The Türkiye-based International Winter Wheat Improvement Program (IWWIP) was set up in 1986 between the Government of Türkiye and CIMMYT; ICARDA joined in 1991. According to an assessment published in 2019, 93 varieties from the programme had been released in 11 countries and were sown on an estimated 2.5-3.0 million hectares a year; the programme distributes its material to more than 100 cooperators on every continent.

The same assessment describes the programme's move to new tools: alongside the long route of the established system, a shorter one opened in 2018 with a speed breeding facility in İzmir that allows two to three generations a year. Until recently the programme remained largely a conventional breeding programme: in 2017 molecular markers were analysed on a germplasm set of more than 1,500, and in 2018 all candidate lines for the international observation nursery were genotyped with markers. NDVI measurement with a hand-held crop sensor is routine in preliminary yield trials; in 2018 a workshop on modern phenotyping tools was held in Konya. The programme's stated future strategy is to combine conventional breeding with precision and high-throughput phenotyping, genomic tools and speed breeding. How genomic selection or AI affects variety development time or genetic gain in a breeding programme in Türkiye, however, has not been measured in a published assessment.

The programme has left a mark on genetic resources too: in 2009-2014 it carried out a national inventory of wheat landraces in Türkiye; more than 60 of the 81 provinces were visited and landraces were collected from more than 1,500 farms.

In academia, machine learning is entering early-stage screening. A study by five universities in Türkiye with Polish partners measured the response of tetraploid wheat genotypes to drought at germination, under stress induced in the laboratory with polyethylene glycol, and modelled it with four machine learning techniques (Benlioğlu and colleagues, 2024). The authors present the method as a fast and low-cost approach; the results belong to the laboratory and the germination stage, not to yield in the field.

The scale of seed itself is large: according to the ministry's data, certified seed production rose by 3.7% in 2025 on the previous year to about 1.35 million tonnes; the largest item is wheat, at 640,829 tonnes.
