TARIM PORTALI · CRITICAL VIEW

Failures and overstatements

https://tarimportali.net/yapay-zeka-dijital-tarim/basarisizliklar-ve-abartilar

The short history of AI in agriculture also includes promises that failed. Of three companies that grew on the promise of AI, Infarm went into administration, AppHarvest filed for bankruptcy protection and Small Robot Company went into liquidation. For harvesting robots, the distance between the lab and the field did not close for decades; an FAO estimate of how much food production must rise was also recalculated. In Türkiye, a World Bank-financed climate-smart agriculture project is falling behind its targets.

Companies that grew on the promise of AI

Infarm. Infarm, which ran vertical farms in cities, announced on 16 December 2021 that it had raised 200 million dollars; according to the company's own statement, it was valued at well over 1 billion dollars. According to the release, each farm was equipped with lab-grade sensors that had collected more than 60 billion data points from the company's global network, and its crop science team analysed the data with AI and its patented technology to improve yield, quality and nutritional value. According to the company's own statement, in the previous three years the analyses made possible by the AI-driven "farm brain" had cut production costs by 80% and raised yields by more than 250%; the new farming model could be as much as 400 times more efficient than soil-based agriculture. The goal was 100 growing centres in 20 countries by 2030.

According to the company's own announcement, Infarm – Indoor Urban Farming GmbH entered administration on 4 September 2023 under the insolvency rules of England and Wales; the UK companies register also records that the administration began on that date. The cost and yield figures in the investment release were the company's own statement, and no published measurement independently tests such claims that companies attribute to AI.

AppHarvest. AppHarvest, a US company growing produce indoors, announced on 8 April 2021 that it had bought Root AI, an agricultural robotics and AI company. According to the company's own statement, the price was about 60 million dollars, about 10 million of it in cash and the rest in AppHarvest shares. The release presented the robot, Virgo, as the world's first universal harvester, able to identify and harvest many crops of different sizes, from tomatoes to strawberries; the company said the real advantage would be using the data the robots collect as they harvest to assess crop health and predict yields precisely.

On 23 July 2023, AppHarvest Products, LLC and eleven affiliated companies filed for protection under Chapter 11 of the US Bankruptcy Code. Even in the release announcing the filing, according to the company's own statement, its farms were designed to use up to 90% less water than open fields and to produce yields up to 30 times those of traditional agriculture.

Small Robot Company. Small Robot Company, a farm robotics start-up in the United Kingdom, announced in 2020 that it had placed an AI-driven advice engine, Wilma, at the heart of its robotic farming system. According to the company's post, the aim was per-plant farming, which the company believed would become the dominant system in the fourth agricultural revolution, and that year it aimed to show that it could kill weeds autonomously without chemicals. The post said Wilma would be commercially available to early customers from October. According to the UK companies register, the company entered creditors' voluntary liquidation on 1 February 2024 and was dissolved on 30 April 2026.

The distance between the lab and the field

According to a review of harvesting robots for high-value crops (Bac and colleagues, 2014), the robots located the fruit with an average success of 85% and detached it with 75%; harvest success stayed at 66%. 5% of the fruit and 45% of the stalks were damaged; a harvest cycle took 33 seconds on average. According to the review, the performance of harvesting robots had not improved in the past three decades, and none of the 50 robots examined had been commercialised.

In recognising disease from images, too, a headline figure and field performance can diverge: in a 2016 study, the model's 99.35% accuracy refers to images taken in controlled conditions; on images taken in other conditions, accuracy falls to just above 31%.

A figure recalculated: 70%

A background paper that FAO prepared in 2009 for an experts' forum wrote that the world's population would reach 9.1 billion by 2050, 34% more than at the time, and that to feed it, food production (net of food used for biofuels) must increase by 70%. FAO revised the estimate in 2017: in 2050 agriculture will need to produce almost 50% more food, feed and biofuel than in 2012. This estimate rested on the UN projections of the time that the world's population would reach 9.73 billion in 2050.

The two figures do not measure the same thing: the 2009 figure covers food production, the 2017 figure food, feed and biofuel; and their starting years differ. A 2012 projection called AT2050 foresaw an increase of 60%; updating only the population projections raises the increase between 2005/07 and 2050 to more than 63%. Because production grew by 15% between 2005/07 and 2012, the remaining increase from 2013 to 2050 is about 49%. So most of the apparent fall from 70% to 50% comes from the different starting years and scopes. In 2017 FAO also wrote that meeting the increased demand should not be a major challenge, if past achievements are a guide: between 1961 and 2011, global agricultural output more than tripled.

Türkiye: a project behind its targets

The loan agreement for the Türkiye Climate Smart and Competitive Agricultural Growth Project (TUCSAP) between Türkiye and the World Bank's International Bank for Reconstruction and Development was signed on 16 May 2022; the loan is 304,800,000 euros. One of the project's sub-components is a digital blueprint for the ministry's collection and management of sectoral information. The project will end on 31 March 2028.

According to an audit report by the Treasury Controllers Board of the Ministry of Treasury and Finance dated 16 June 2026, 12.91% of the loan had been spent by the end of 2025. For 2025 the original budget was 108.8 million euros and the final budget 55.6 million euros; spending in the year was 27.9 million euros, 50.17% of the final budget. In the grant category, where a total of 29.5 million euros is planned, nothing had been spent by the end of 2025. According to the World Bank's November 2024 monitoring report, the March 2028 target for the indicator on farmers adopting climate-smart agricultural technologies is 42,500; in October 2024 the value was zero, and the bank writes that reporting on the indicator is delayed because of delays in deploying the grant financing.
