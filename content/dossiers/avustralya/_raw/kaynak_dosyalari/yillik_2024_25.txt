Victorian Water Trading: 2024-25 
1. About this report 
The purpose of this report is to provide a record of water trade behaviour across the year in Victoria. It 
maintains continuity with annual water trading reports dating back to 2007-08. 
Many terms and phrases have specific meanings in the context of Victoria’s water market. You can learn 
more about the types of water rights that can be traded at Water Trading - Water Register. A water dictionary 
is also available for definitions of terms. 
Section 4: Additional tables and charts presents supplementary data without written commentary and is 
provided as additional context to the analysis in this report. 
This report is a streamlined version of what was released in previous annual trade reports. Unless otherwise 
stated, the data presented in this report comes from the Victorian Water Register. If you wish to explore 
more, the Victorian Water Register website has data available to view or download, or you can contact us on 
water.reporting@deeca.vic.gov.au.

The southern Murray-Darling basin saw drying conditions in 2024-25, with total water availability falling 
somewhat in Victoria. At the same time there was a record volume of allocation trade (excluding 
environmental trade) for the fourth year in a row. Allocation trade prices were generally higher but more 
stable than the previous year. 
3. 2024-25 Key trade statistics 
Private allocation trade volume continued to grow 
• 4,062 GL of allocation was traded in Victoria in 2024-25, the 2nd highest on record. This was slightly 
less than 4,177 GL in the previous year (Figure 1). The decrease in total trade was due to less 
environmental trade.

• Excluding environmental trade, there was more trade of all other priced and $0-priced allocation 
(2,684 GL) compared to the previous year (2,467 GL). This continues the long-term trend that has 
seen the scale of the Victorian allocation market consistently increase in size. 
• Total allocation trade in Southern Victoria (19 GL) was higher than the previous year (10 GL), this is in 
contrast with declining trade volumes over the previous 6 years (Figure 8). Within the Southern 
systems, Werribee saw a doubling in volume traded (from 1.5 to 3 GL traded), and 
Thomson/Macalister saw much more priced trade occur compared to last year (Table 3). 
Net trade out of Victoria has occurred for the 2nd year in a row, opposite the historical norm of net imports 
into Victoria 
• Private (non-environmental) net trade to South Australia was about the same, but private net trade 
to NSW increased (Figure 7). Demand for irrigation in South Australia and drying conditions in 2024-
25 for both NSW and South Australia may be contributing factors. 
• The overall movement of private water within Victoria was similar to the previous year, with trade 
from Victorian Tributaries1 to Zone 7 (138 GL) about the same the previous year (152 GL). 
• The largest change in intervalley trade is an increase in private (non-environmental) exports to NSW, 
most notably from Zone 7 to NSW Murrumbidgee which had seen trade into Zone 7 of between 0 GL 
and 41 GL over the past 6 years but instead exported 12 GL to NSW Murrumbidgee in 2024-25 (Figure 
11).

Victorian Water Trading: 2024-25 
Allocation prices were higher and consistent across most of the year 
• Median allocation prices in Northern Victoria for 2024-25 started higher than the same time in the 
previous year ($100-150/ML compared to $80-100/ML respectively) (Figure 2). 
• Where allocation prices peaked up to $200/ML in October then fell throughout 2023-24, prices in 
2024-25 were broadly stable during the first half of the year, before rising in April and May 2025. 
• In Southern Victoria, median allocation prices in the Werribee system increased to $400/ML which is 
a return to the long-term average. Thompson/Macalister saw allocation prices decrease slightly from 
$200/ML to $166/ML (Figure 10).

Commercial trade was the most common reason for trade by volume 
• Standard commercial trades made up 43% of all non-environmental trade volume in 2024-25, 
similar to the 42% in the previous year (Table 4). 
• The median price for trades as part of forward contracts fell slightly compared to the previous year 
from $140/ML to $125/ML across Northern Victoria, which is below the $135/ML for standard 
commercial trades. 
• The volume of allocation traded under forward contacts and private lease agreements (not LTT) has 
increased each year since 2020-21 up to 159 GL in 2024-25. This growth indicates a growing number 
of irrigators are seeking to mitigate the risk of price fluctuations by utilising these arrangements. 
• Unlike in June 2024, median prices of carry over parking arrangements in June 2025 ($20/ML) were 
much lower than standard commercial trades (between $150-225/ML) in Northern Victoria. Lower 
water availability compared to 2023-24 results in less unused water and more available carryover 
space, contributing to the lower carry over prices observed at the end of the 2024-25 (Figure 12 and 
Figure 13).

Victorian Water Trading: 2024-25 
Water share trade volume was higher in Northern Victoria 
• Northern Victoria saw 72 GL of high-reliability and 40 GL of low-reliability water shares transferred, 
which was about 15% higher than in 2022-23 (60 GL and 30 GL respectively) (Figure 3). 
• The Australian Government commenced its Voluntary Water Purchase Program in July 2024 and 
water share transfers in 2024-25 included 14 GL of high-reliability and 3 GL of low-reliability 
purchased under the Murray-Darling Basin Plan. You can read more about Victoria’s response to 
these buybacks at https://www.water.vic.gov.au/our-programs/murray-darling-basin/victorian-
basin-committee and see up-to-date data at Australian Government purchases – Water Register. 
• Southern Victoria saw 4 GL of high-reliability and 2 GL of low-reliability water share transfers in 2024-
25, which was about half as much volume as in 2023-24 but across a similar number of transfers. 
• Water share prices in 2024-25 were broadly similar to the previous year in Southern Victoria and 7 
VIC Murray – Barmah to SA. There was a with a small decrease in the annual median price in 6 VIC 
Murray – Dart to Barmah to $4,250/ML from $4,500/ML in 2023-24, with a similarly change also in 1A 
Greater Goulburn (Figure 11).

Temporary surface water take and use licence trade is higher 
• There was an increase in temporary and permanent trade in 2024-25 compared to the previous year, 
with temporary trade volume being the highest since 2018-19. Trade as part of land transfers was 
about the same (Figure 4). 
• Unlike other trade types, the largest volume of surface water take and use licence trade is recorded 
in Southern Victoria, this is in line with the volume of surface water take and use licences on issue 
being greatest in Southern Victoria.

Temporary groundwater trade is up, but permanent transfers are down 
• Temporary trade of groundwater take and use licences rose compared to the previous year, but the 
volume of permanent trade was about half (Figure 5). 
• Northern Victoria was the most active, with Southern Victoria following. Western Victoria 
experienced the smallest number and volume of trades in all trade categories, the same as in 
previous years. This aligns with the total volume of groundwater entitlements on issue in each 
region.

Extraction share trade is minimal 
• In November 2023 the ‘General Place of Take’ framework passed into law establishing ‘Extraction 
Share’ in declared systems as a right to take water during water restrictions. 
• There is not developed market for extraction share yet with only 17 market trades recorded in 2024-
25. To learn more about place of take and extraction share see the water register website here: Place 
of take approvals - Water Register 
Limited Term Transfers total active volume continues declining since 2019-20 
• As some Limited Term Transfers (LTTs) expired the total volume of active LTTs on 30 June 2025 
decreased by 9% to 123 GL and 28 GL for both high and low-reliability LTTs respectively (Figure 6). 
• The volume of new LTTs recorded in 2024-25 was 13 GL, about the same as 15 GL in the previous 
year (Figure 16Figure 6). 
• The volume of LTTs active in 2024-25 in Southern Victoria was unchanged from the previous year at 2 
GL and 1 GL for high and low-reliability LTTs respectively.
