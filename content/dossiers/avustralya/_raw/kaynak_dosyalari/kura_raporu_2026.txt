Post Trade Report – July 2026 
 
 
This report provides a summary of water allocation trade activities for the July 2026 Barmah and Goulburn-to-Murray 
trade opening events. It provides insight into the operation and outcomes of the trade opportunities under the 
Goulburn-to-Murray and Barmah Narrows trading rules. 
 
The report is divided into two sections: the first section covers the Barmah trade opening, and the second part covers 
the Goulburn-to-Murray trade opening. 
 
Application process summary 
Allocation trade applications were submitted between 7:00 am and 2:00 pm on 2 July 2026. After the application 
window closed, eligible applications were randomised to determine the order in which they would be processed for 
the Goulburn -to-Murray and Barma h Narrows trade openings. DEECA and WaterNSW jointly undertook the 
randomisation of eligible Barmah Narrows applications. Both randomisation processes were independently observed 
by the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC). 
 
Victorian trade applications for the opening were submitted through My Water, DEECA’s online platform for accessing 
the Victorian Water Register and trading water allocation. For the purposes of this report, Victorian application 
sources are reported as either Broker Portal (including Broker API connections) or Online Trading (all other registered 
My Water users). 
 
The randomisation approach has been implemented as an interim solution to manage disruptions to trade openings. 
The approach reduces pressure on Victorian Water Register systems during trade openings and maintains the 
integrity of the trade application processing queue. Longer term options for improving the efficiency and equity of 
access to inter-valley trade opportunities are being considered under a joint project between DEECA and WaterNSW. 
DEECA has sought input from the public and industry stakeholders and will publish findings later in 2026. Information 
regarding the project is available on Engage Victoria. 
 
Barmah trade opening – 2 July 2026 
Barmah Narrows trade rule 
The Barmah Narrows trade rule restricts downstream trade through the Barmah Narrows. The Barmah Narrows is a 
naturally narrow point on the River Murray, which restricts the river flow to just around 7,000 ML per day. Due to this 
flow restriction, a trade restriction is in place on downstream trade to protect delivery to existing entitlement holders 
and to maintain the river and riparian environment in and around the Narrows. The restriction means that trade 
downstream of the Barmah Narrows may only occur when there is sufficient matching trade capacity available in the 
opposite direction. 
At the end of each water trading year on the 30th of June, under normal operations, the balance available for trading 
from above to below the Barmah Narrows is cleared. Then, at the beginning of next water year (1 July), a volume is 
credited to the Barmah trade balance. This annual adjustment account for water savings downstream of the Narrows 
which have been transferred to the Snowy scheme. This initial volume is available to be traded from above to below 
the Barmah Narrows. The Murray-Darling Basin Authority (MDBA) manages the limit as part of its role of managing 
the River Murray on behalf of New South Wales and Victoria under the Murray-Darling Basin Agreement.

Goulburn-to-Murray trade opening – 2 July 2026 
Goulburn-to-Murray trade rule 
The Goulburn-to-Murray trade rule governs the trade of water allocations from the Goulburn, Broken, Campaspe and 
Loddon systems to the Victorian Murray system and interstate. 
The rule helps to protect the health of the lower Goulburn River, by keeping trade opportunity in line with what can 
sustainably be delivered under the lower Goulburn operating rules . This means that traded water is delivered in a 
more sustainable, variable pattern, providing lower and more natural flows over summer and autumn. The trade rule 
continues to support water users by enabling the trade and movement of water, within limits that seek to improve 
river health over the long-term. 
The trade rule allows for 240 GL of trade out, less legacy trade reserves, plus seasonal trade opportunity. New trade 
opportunity is made available through three specific announcements on 1 July, 15 October , and 15 December (or 
the next business day). Available seasonal trade opportunity is linked to real water use in the Murray River, allowing 
more flexibility to respond to seasonal condition s. Opportunity can also be released due to adjustments to legacy 
trade commitments. 
Possible volumes for each trade opportunity are determined in accordance with the Trading Rules for Declared 
Water Systems. You can read in more detail about the trade rule at the VWR website and you can read worked 
examples of how the trade opportunity is calculated, for dry, average and wet years.
