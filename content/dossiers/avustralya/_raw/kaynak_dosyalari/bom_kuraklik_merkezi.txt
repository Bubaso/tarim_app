Declaring a drought

As an agency of the Australian Government, the Bureau does not declare a
drought. The Australian Government does not make drought declarations.

We provide drought and climate information that supports government-led
drought policy, preparation and response.

State and territory governments administer and coordinate drought policies
and programs for their jurisdiction.

The approach and response to drought is guided by the National Drought
Agreement and the Australian Government Drought Plan.

What is drought?

Drought is a prolonged, abnormally dry period when there's not enough water
available for normal use. It's not simply low rainfall - if it was, much of
inland Australia would almost always be considered in drought.

We generally measure drought conditions over at least 3 months. But it can be
measured over weeks or longer, which helps categorise the type of drought:
weeks - flash drought
months - meteorological drought
up to several years - hydrological drought.
