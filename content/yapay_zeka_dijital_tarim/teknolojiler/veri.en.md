# Data platforms

The value of farm data emerges not in a single field but when the data of thousands of fields are pooled; AI in agriculture is trained on that accumulation. Free access multiplied the use of public satellite data: after Landsat imagery was made free in 2008, data use rose **20**-fold according to NASA. Farm data, by contrast, do not flow without trust: in a choice experiment, on average **36%** of farmers were willing to share their data with a big data platform. To the question of whose the data are, Europe answers with rights rather than ownership. In Türkiye the largest register of farm data is held by the state, and no rule governs farmers' access to their own data or its portability.

## Trust decides sharing

In Turland and Slade's (2020) experiment, who ran the platform was decisive: farmers were most willing to share their data with university researchers and least willing with government. Yet small benefits markedly raised participation, even among farmers with strong privacy preferences. The agreements show how data actually flow: of the **141** agricultural app agreements examined by Kaur and Dara (2023), **95%** fall into the difficult-to-read category and close to **75%** require university-level education to understand. For public data the fee decides: the USGS expects more than **50%** of Landsat users to decline to pay if charged.

## From ownership to rights of access

The 2018 code of conduct of Europe's farmer and agri-industry organisations accepts that data cannot be owned like physical assets and gives the rights to the farmer: no use or sharing without the originator's consent, and the right to receive one's own data in machine-readable form. But access to machine data stays restricted, and compliance is voluntary. According to Atik (2022), the ownership framing does not solve the real problems, such as data lock-in, data fragmentation and farmers' lack of trust, and could worsen them.

A binding regulation has now been added alongside the voluntary code. Under the EU Data Act, since 12 September 2025 users have had a right of access to the data their connected machines generate; connected products placed on the market after 12 September 2026 must be designed to make these data available by default, free of charge and in a machine-readable format. Its recitals add that the data holder should not use a farm's performance data against the farmer when negotiating to buy its produce. According to the Commission, common European data spaces are being developed across **14** sectors, with eight initiatives in agriculture. Tzachor and colleagues (2022) propose data cooperatives for data transparency and ownership rights.

## The register is held by the state

In Türkiye the backbone of farm data is public registers. The farmer registry that the World Bank calls Türkiye's basic rural database was created together with direct income support after a pilot in the 2000 agricultural year. The area registered in the Farmer Registration System (ÇKS) reached **16.1** million hectares in 2025, **93%** of the **17.3** million-hectare target. The General Agricultural Census entered about **3.4** million hectares not registered in ministry systems into the register ex officio; the 2027 Programme foresees a system registering every farm holding with spatial data, and a national open data portal. In the fruit and vegetable trade, the Wholesale Market Registration System is a central register that issues a uniquely numbered tag for every notification. Its data are also used in research: a machine learning trial built a new dataset by compiling **1,791** days of wholesale prices and trade volumes by hand from the Ministry of Trade's website.

The Türkiye AI Action Plan (2026-2030) aims for data spaces in which companies and public institutions share data under common rules and standard contracts, a sectoral roadmap for agriculture, and at least **2,000** public datasets published in machine-readable form; but it does not address data ownership and never mentions farmers. Under the definition in Law No. **6698** on the Protection of Personal Data, parcel or production data that can be linked to a farmer may count as personal data. Nor is there a published study measuring Turkish farmers' willingness to share data.
