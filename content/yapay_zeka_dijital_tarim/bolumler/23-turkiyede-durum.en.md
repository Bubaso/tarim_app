# 23 · Türkiye today

> **Summary.** Agricultural AI in Türkiye runs on two separate tracks. The Türkiye AI Action Plan (2026-2030) counts agriculture among its priority sectors, yet in none of the plan's **16** actions is the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry either the lead or a partner institution. Agriculture's own AI policy sits in the Development Plan and the annual programmes, under the ministry's responsibility. The only concrete AI task in the 2027 Annual Programme is forecasting drought **1-4 weeks** ahead, and it is written as something that is planned (planlanmaktadır). Outside the programme, units of the ministry use AI, or are preparing to, in three monitoring tasks: identifying crops from satellites, decision support against forest fires and tracking fishing vessels. No published assessment measuring the accuracy or effect of any of them was found. The ministry's research arm, TAGEM, started three AI projects in 2025; the ministry's own AI strategy, meanwhile, has not yet been published.

## The national plan: agriculture in, ministry out

The previous plan, the 2024-2025 Action Plan of the National AI Strategy, did not mention agriculture in any of its **71** actions. It named **11** ministries as lead or related institutions; the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry was not among them.

The 2026-2030 plan changed that picture. Agriculture is now one of four sectors whose data and regulatory needs will be defined first: health, agriculture, defence and e-commerce. Agricultural sites are listed among the places where pilots will run; the physical AI and robotics programme includes farm robots; agriculture is one of the priority areas of the foundation-model research calls; joint pilot programmes with the D-8 countries cover agriculture too. The plan uses the stem "tarım" (agriculture) **19** times; once repetitions and two general opening sentences are set aside, **11** distinct contexts remain.

What did not change is the institution. In the new plan every action names a lead institution and its partner institutions. Nine ministries appear in those lists; the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry appears in none. The words "çiftçi" (farmer) and "kırsal" (rural) occur in neither plan. Agriculture enters the plan as a field where AI will be applied, not with its own institution or its own subject.

## Agriculture's own track: the Development Plan and the annual programme

The only agricultural policy in the 2027 Annual Programme that carries the words "yapay zekâ" (artificial intelligence) comes from paragraph **489** of the Twelfth Development Plan, and the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry is responsible for it. The policy has two activities written for 2027: a shared machinery pool in **14** producer organisations holding first-tier certificates, and the roll-out of a domestically developed Secure Electronic Ear Tag and Tracking System. Neither description mentions AI.

Information and communication technologies in irrigation are a separate policy (paragraph **505**). The Digital Irrigation Management System project (DİSU) is to be completed; automation and decision-support systems are to be put into operation at existing irrigation facilities.

The programme's only concrete AI task sits not in the table of measures but in the description of the current situation. The Agricultural Drought Monitoring System (TAKİS) tracks drought with satellite data at **7**-day intervals; in **2027**, work is planned to begin on forecasting drought conditions **1-4 weeks** ahead using AI methods.

The Rural Development Investment Programme lists AI as a priority: it aims to spread digitalisation, artificial intelligence, smart agriculture, robotics and automation technologies. Grants cover **50-70%** of the project amount; at least **20%** of the programme budget is reserved for women and young entrepreneurs and **30%** for family farms. The programme itself does not say how much of that budget goes to AI.

None of the **15** performance indicators at the end of the agriculture chapter measures digitalisation, smart agriculture or AI. The AI policy has no measurable target for 2027.

## Outside the programme: systems at work in the field

The annual programme mentions AI in a single forecasting task. The announcements and documents of the ministry's own units, however, show three more systems that use AI or machine learning, or are preparing to. All three do the same kind of work: watching over a large area or a large number of objects.

The General Directorate of Agrarian Reform put its remote-sensing crop identification work into practice for the first time in the 2025 production year: reference parcels collected in the field are assessed together with satellite imagery, and machine learning is used to detect and classify the crops sown (§9). **12** regional meetings were held in July 2026; checks on the accuracy of farmer registry (ÇKS) declarations are planned within the same work. The announcement says areas are analysed with high accuracy rates but gives no rate.

Since 2022 the General Directorate of Forestry has used a Decision Support System that combines meteorological data, forest information system data, UAV and satellite images; according to the ministry, it predicts a fire's behaviour and spread with AI support (§15).

The Fishing Vessel Monitoring System (BAGİS) tracks the position of commercial fishing vessels of **12** metres and over via satellite and GSM. According to the ministry's technical requirements document, published on TÜBİTAK's website in June 2026, data for **1,629** vessels are current and accessible; but for now these data are used mainly for monitoring and record keeping, and checks on prohibited areas are done by staff at screens. The document describes a project in which the data will be analysed with AI to detect possible violations (§14).

In a May 2025 speech published on the ministry's website, the minister also said that AI-supported remote sensing was their greatest aid in production planning and that they used AI-supported technologies against forest fires. This scan found no published assessment independently measuring the accuracy or effect of any of the three systems.

## The ministry's own document: pending

The preparation of the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry's AI-Supported Digital Transformation Strategy and Action Plan can be followed step by step on the ministry's own website. In the same May 2025 speech, noting that the Fourth Agriculture and Forestry Council had made important findings on AI, the minister listed their plans: a research institute for research on digital agriculture, AI and advanced agricultural technologies, early warning systems against drought and extreme weather and a digital drought monitoring system, the use of satellite technologies and AI in agricultural insurance, an agricultural observation satellite network, and AI-supported forecasting and monitoring systems for agricultural production. On 23-24 October 2025 a workshop based on the Council's decisions was held, hosted by the General Directorate of Agrarian Reform; as groundwork for the strategy and action plan to be prepared, it set out AI-supported digital transformation action proposals for crop production, livestock, rural development, forestry and water management. Experts from Gartner contributed to the proposals alongside experts from the public sector, academia and the private sector.

The document was presented on 24 November 2025, during the 2026 budget hearing, as a document to be shared at the beginning of the year. On 4 June 2026 it was announced at a summit that the document had been prepared; this comes from a news report. As of November 2026 its text has not been published. Nor did this scan find a primary text announcing that the research institute the minister mentioned has been established.

This heading will be rewritten when the document is published. Until then, the ministry's view of AI in agriculture can be read from the annual programmes, the announcements of its units and statements.

## The research side: TAGEM

The 2025 activity report of the ministry's research arm, the General Directorate of Agricultural Research and Policies (TAGEM), published in January 2026, lists digital agriculture and AI research among its research areas; the 2026-2030 Agricultural Research Master Plan has a separate Digital Agriculture and AI Research Opportunity Area.

The report describes three AI projects that started in 2025. The first aims to detect important diseases and pests in some fruit species and in vineyards with deep learning: an image dataset is to be built in cooperation between **8** research institutes under TAGEM and **2** universities, and the best-performing model is to be offered to fruit growers and experts as web and mobile software. The second is a prototype device to determine the sulphur dioxide and moisture content of dried apricots with multispectral imaging and deep learning. The third is a prototype precision sprayer for maize and cotton that locates weeds with cameras and opens its nozzles only over the target (§8). The report describes all three in the future tense, as goals, and gives no results.

According to the report, the VetAlarm project, which is to develop an AI-based early diagnosis and warning system for animal diseases, was also approved in 2025. The Plant Protection Central Research Institute under TAGEM, meanwhile, is a partner in a project for an autonomous greenhouse robot able to fertilise, spray and harvest using AI and image processing; the project was found worthy of support under TÜBİTAK's AI Ecosystem Call, started on 1 January 2026 and is planned to be completed at the end of 2027.

## The layer beneath the AI

The record-keeping and measurement layer that AI would be built on is more concrete in the programme, even though it is not AI. The B-Prescription System, which keeps prescriptions for plant protection products in electronic form, has been in use in **81** provinces since 1 July 2026. A Farm Holding Registration System is to link every agricultural holding to spatial data, using the General Agricultural Census; a data centre for the agricultural information systems is to be completed. Detailed soil surveys and mapping of **3.5 million** hectares of farmland in **15** provinces will continue. Electronic metering is to be installed at **100** facilities so that water can be priced by the amount used.

None of this is AI. But it is the data a model would learn from, the measurement a forecast would rest on and the record a decision would be written into.

What this dossier's scan did not find is **a published independent assessment measuring the accuracy or field effect of any of the ministry's AI-supported systems.**
