# 22 · The world, region by region

> **Summary.** The spread of digital agriculture around the world cannot be ranked on a single scale; each region counts something different. In the US, **22%** of farms reported using some precision agriculture practice in 2025, while according to a 2023 USDA report automated guidance was used on more than half of the planted acreage of the main crops. In the EU, about **18%** of farms use precision farming, but these farms work about **44%** of the farmland. In China, more than **3.5** million satellite positioning devices had been fitted to farm machinery in total by the end of 2025, and the farm drone fleet exceeds **300,000**. In India, more than **103** million farmers have been given a digital ID, and an AI chatbot has answered more than **9.3** million questions. In Israel the measure is companies, not farms: according to Start-Up Nation Central's own announcement, there are more than **750** companies in agriculture and food technology. In Africa, only **42%** of the more than **33** million farmers registered with digital agriculture services used them with any frequency.

## Why the figures cannot be set side by side

The regions measure digital agriculture in different units. The US farm survey and the EU count the share of farms using a technology; the US economic research service counts the share of planted acreage; China's agriculture ministry counts its own composite indicator; India counts administrative records; the African report counts registered and actually active farmers; and Israel counts companies and capital. The figures below therefore yield no ranking. AI itself is mostly not measured separately either: the US survey asks about precision agriculture practices, and the EU farm structure survey about internet, management information systems, robotics and precision farming; India counts the questions answered by a chatbot, and China counts drones and satellite positioning devices.

## United States: share of farms versus share of acreage

According to the US Department of Agriculture's statistics service (NASS), **85%** of farms had internet access in 2025; **55%** used broadband and **74%** a mobile data plan. The share of farms reporting the use of a precision agriculture practice in the past twelve months was **27%** in 2023 and **22%** in 2025. The question covers GPS guidance, yield monitoring, variable-rate application, drones, electronic tagging, precision feeding and robotic milking; the survey covers about **28,000** operations.

The department's economic research service (ERS) measures the same technologies by planted acreage. According to its 2023 report, yield maps, soil maps and variable-rate application are used on only **5-25%** of the planted acreage of winter wheat, cotton, sorghum and rice, while automated guidance is used on more than half of the acreage of six major crops. Auto-steer was used on **5.3%** of planted corn acreage in 2001 and **58%** in 2016. The gap between large and small farms is wide: at least half of large farms rely on one of these technologies, while less than **25%** of the smallest farms use any of them. One example of federal support is conservation payments: in 2020, precision pesticide application was contracted on more than **2.24** million acres under these payments, the most common of the programme's digital-agriculture-related enhancements. According to the report's authors, several federal programmes that directly affect digital agriculture have been introduced in recent years, but no major study documents them; lack of broadband is one of the main barriers to adoption.

## European Union: a small share, a large area

According to Eurostat's July 2026 analysis based on the 2023 farm structure survey, about **43%** of EU farms have internet access; in Denmark, Germany, Slovakia, Latvia, Czechia and Austria the rate is above **90%**. One farm in ten uses a farm management information system, and robotics is used on about **7%**. About **18%** of farms apply a precision farming technique; the most common is soil analysis (about **11%**), followed by variable-rate application (about **5%**) and band spraying (about **4%**). But these farms manage about **44%** of the EU's farmland. **74%** of the farms with the largest economic size have internet access, against about **21%** of the smallest. According to Eurostat, the main barriers are high upfront costs and limited digital skills; most farmers buy new technology with their own resources.

In the common agricultural policy (2023-2027), digitalisation is a cross-cutting goal together with knowledge and innovation. According to the Commission's implementation highlights, more than **20,000** farms received support to adopt digital technologies; in the same period income support reached **5.6** million farms. In its vision for agriculture and food, the Commission proposed an EU-wide digital strategy for agriculture.

## China: a state plan and machine scale

In October 2024, China's Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs issued guidance on smart agriculture and a 2024-2028 national smart agriculture action plan. The ministry's own composite indicator, the informatisation rate of agricultural production, was **27.6%** in 2022; the target is about **35%** by 2030 and more than **40%** by 2035. The ministry writes that, in key technologies and equipment as well as in the breadth and depth of application, China still lags considerably behind the US and Europe.

According to a report published in August 2026 by the Cyberspace Administration of China and the ministry, rural internet penetration rose from **38.4%** at the end of 2018 to **69.5%** at the end of 2025, and the urban-rural gap narrowed from **36.2** to **15.6** points. By the end of 2025, more than **3.5** million BeiDou satellite positioning devices had been deployed on farm machinery in total, and the farm drone fleet exceeded **300,000**; **34** smart agriculture innovation centres and sub-centres and **116** application projects had been set up. In 2024, **50.57%** of rural adults had basic or higher digital skills.

## India: IDs and a chatbot

In 2024 India launched a Digital Agriculture Mission with a budget of **2,817** crore rupees (**1** crore is **10** million). Its backbone is AgriStack: each farmer is given an ID by matching farmers' details with digitised land records. As of 3 August 2026, **10.31** crore, or more than **103** million, farmer IDs had been created; the target for 2026-27 is **11** crore. Farmer registries are kept at state level and the states own the data; AI and machine learning are among the methods envisaged for yield estimation on this infrastructure.

According to a government note of February 2026, Kisan e-Mitra, a voice-enabled AI chatbot launched in 2023, answers farmers' questions about government schemes: by December 2025 it had answered more than **93** lakh, or **9.3** million, questions, and it handles more than **8,000** questions a day in **11** regional languages. Probabilistic forecasts of local monsoon onset were sent by text message to more than **3.88** crore (**38.8** million) farmers in **13** states; according to follow-up surveys in two states, **31-52%** of farmers changed their sowing decisions based on the forecasts.

## Israel: companies, not farms

In Israel digital agriculture is measured through the technology sector rather than farms. According to the Israel Innovation Authority's 2025 report, the country has more than **1,500** active deep-tech companies, and Israeli deep-tech companies in medical devices, agriculture and food attract about **9-10%** of global investment in these fields. But only **2.4%** of new technology companies founded in 2022-2024 are in agricultural technology. The Authority counts agricultural technology among the sectors with low or medium access to private capital; it directs about **23%** of its own investments to food, agriculture, water and energy, while these sectors receive **4%** of private early-stage investment. According to its own announcement, Start-Up Nation Central, which promotes Israel's start-up ecosystem, counts more than **750** companies in agriculture and food technology, with more than **30%** of investment going to robotics and farm equipment.

## Africa: the gap between registered and active

In Africa, one of the comprehensive continent-wide counts is the 2019 report by CTA and Dalberg. According to it, there were at least **390** active digital agriculture solutions on the continent, and they had registered more than **33** million smallholder farmers and pastoralists. But only **42%** of those registered used the services with any frequency; the share of highly active users was probably in the **15-30%** range. About **20%** of registrations were duplicates; the number of unique users was about **26** million. Around **175** million euros of donor funding flowed into the field each year, while private investment was about **47** million euros in 2018.

The continental AI strategy adopted by the African Union in July 2024 counts agriculture, together with health and education, among the three priority sectors for AI adoption. The strategy mentions small-scale use cases such as crop disease diagnosis, price prediction, expert advice and access to financial services, and proposes a centre of excellence to document use cases in agriculture. Internet use in Africa rose from **20%** in 2015 to **40%** in 2023.
