# 24 · Regulation and policy

> **Summary.** The rules that govern AI in agriculture do not come from a separate farm law; they sit where vehicle, machinery, data and general AI legislation meet. The framework Türkiye follows most closely is the EU's. The EU AI Act treats AI that performs a safety function in a farm vehicle or a machine as high-risk, but a July 2026 amendment postponed those rules to **2 August 2028**. The text that matters most to farmers today is the Data Act: since **12 September 2025**, users have a right of access to the data their connected machines generate. Türkiye has no AI-specific law; its national plan foresees a framework that takes account of the EU's proportionate risk approach.

## The EU AI Act: where agriculture comes in

| What | Applies from | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Prohibited practices and general provisions | 2 February 2025 | Regulation 2024/1689 |
| Obligations for general-purpose models | 2 August 2025 | Regulation 2024/1689 |
| General application | 2 August 2026 | Regulation 2024/1689 |
| High-risk systems listed in Annex III | 2 December 2027 | Amending Regulation 2026/1744 |
| High-risk systems that are safety components of a product (Annex I) | 2 August 2028 | Amending Regulation 2026/1744 |

Agriculture enters the Act through Annex I. AI counts as high-risk when it is a safety component of a product covered by the legislation listed in Annex I. The list includes Regulation 167/2013 on the approval and market surveillance of agricultural and forestry vehicles; since July 2026 the Machinery Regulation (2023/1230) sits in the same section. For products in that section the AI Act's requirements apply not directly but through the product's own legislation: delegated acts adopted for agricultural vehicles must take the Act's high-risk requirements into account for AI systems that are safety components.

The amending regulation (2026/1744) was published in the EU Official Journal on 24 July 2026 and entered into force three days later. Its reasons are stated in the text: the delayed availability of standards, common specifications and guidance, and the delayed establishment of national competent authorities. The AI requirements to be added to the Machinery Regulation are also to apply by 2 August 2028.

The upshot: binding EU requirements for AI that performs a safety function in a farm vehicle or a machine will not apply before 2028. The Act applies to providers placing AI systems on the Union market wherever they are established; Turkish manufacturers selling AI-equipped machinery or farm vehicles into the EU are bound by the same timetable.

## The EU Data Act: whose are the machine's data

The Data Act (2023/2854) has applied since 12 September 2025. Its recitals explicitly list "agricultural and industrial machinery" among connected products. Connected products placed on the market after 12 September 2026 must be designed so that the data they generate are, by default, accessible to the user easily, securely, free of charge and in a machine-readable format.

The Act's recitals name the most concrete concern in farming: the company holding the data should not use knowledge about a farm's overall performance against the farmer when negotiating to buy the farmer's produce, nor use it to feed crop-yield databases for the upcoming harvest. The same recitals cite the code of conduct on agricultural data sharing by contract as a precedent.

## The EU Machinery Regulation: safety components that learn

The Machinery Regulation (2023/1230) applies from 14 January 2027. It assigns the conformity assessment of safety components and systems whose behaviour evolves through machine learning to an independent third party. Software that does not learn, and only executes the automated functions it was programmed for, falls outside that scope. The Regulation also defines autonomous mobile machinery and the supervisor who monitors it remotely.

## Türkiye: a framework on the way, no separate annex for agriculture

This dossier's November 2026 search found no AI-specific law published in the Official Gazette. Action 4 of the Türkiye AI Action Plan (2026-2030) aims to build a regulatory environment that protects citizens' rights and gives investors predictability. Its basis is to be a national legal-ethical framework that takes account of the European Union's proportionate risk approach, with obligations graded by risk level. The targets for the first 12 months are a proportionate-risk guide with sector annexes and a National AI Ethics Board. The plan also aims to set up regulatory sandboxes in at least five priority sectors.

The plan does not say which sectors will get annexes or where the sandboxes will be. The lead institutions for the action are the Ministry of Industry and Technology and the Cybersecurity Directorate; the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry is not on the list of partners, which names sectoral regulators only in general terms. The Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry's own AI strategy has not yet been published (see Türkiye today).
