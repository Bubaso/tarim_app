# 24 · Regulation and policy

> **Summary.** The rules that govern AI in agriculture do not come from a separate farm law; they sit where vehicle, machinery, data and general AI legislation meet. The framework Türkiye follows most closely is the EU's. The EU AI Act treats AI that performs a safety function in a farm vehicle or a machine as high-risk, but a July 2026 amendment postponed those rules to **2 August 2028**. The text that matters most to farmers today is the Data Act: since **12 September 2025**, users have a right of access to the data their connected machines generate. Rules that never mention AI play a part too: since 2023 the EU's common agricultural policy has required member states to run an area monitoring system based on satellite data. Türkiye has no AI-specific law; its national plan foresees a framework that takes account of the EU's proportionate risk approach, while the technical requirements of one of the ministry's AI projects demand compliance with the personal data protection law.

## The EU AI Act: where agriculture comes in

| What | Applies from | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Prohibited practices and general provisions | 2 February 2025 | Regulation 2024/1689 |
| Obligations for general-purpose models | 2 August 2025 | Regulation 2024/1689 |
| General application | 2 August 2026 | Regulation 2024/1689 |
| High-risk systems listed in Annex III | 2 December 2027 | Amending Regulation 2026/1744 |
| High-risk systems that are safety components of a product (Annex I) | 2 August 2028 | Amending Regulation 2026/1744 |

Agriculture enters the Act through Annex I. AI counts as high-risk when it is a safety component of a product covered by the legislation listed in Annex I. The list includes Regulation 167/2013 on the approval and market surveillance of agricultural and forestry vehicles; since July 2026 the Machinery Regulation (2023/1230) sits in the same section. For products in that section the AI Act's requirements apply not directly but through the product's own legislation: delegated acts adopted for agricultural vehicles must take the Act's high-risk requirements into account for AI systems that are safety components.

The amending regulation (2026/1744) was published in the EU Official Journal on 24 July 2026 and entered into force three days later. The amending regulation gives its reasons: the delayed availability of standards, common specifications and guidance, and the delayed establishment of national competent authorities. The AI requirements to be added to the Machinery Regulation are also to apply by 2 August 2028.

The upshot: the AI Act's high-risk requirements for AI that performs a safety function in a farm vehicle or a machine will not apply before 2028, while the Machinery Regulation's third-party assessment requirement for safety components with self-evolving behaviour based on machine learning starts on 14 January 2027 (below). The AI Act applies to providers placing AI systems on the Union market wherever they are established; Turkish manufacturers selling AI-equipped machinery or farm vehicles into the EU are bound by the same timetable.

## The EU Data Act: whose are the machine's data

The Data Act (2023/2854) has applied since 12 September 2025. Its recitals explicitly list "agricultural and industrial machinery" among connected products. Connected products placed on the market after 12 September 2026 must be designed so that the data they generate are, by default, accessible to the user easily, securely, free of charge and in a machine-readable format.

The Act's recitals name the most concrete concern in farming: the company holding the data should not use knowledge about a farm's overall performance against the farmer when negotiating to buy the farmer's produce, nor use it to feed crop-yield databases for the upcoming harvest. The same recitals cite the code of conduct on agricultural data sharing by contract as a precedent.

## The EU Machinery Regulation: safety components that learn

The Machinery Regulation (2023/1230) applies from 14 January 2027. It assigns the conformity assessment of safety components and systems whose behaviour evolves through machine learning to an independent third party. Software that does not learn, and only executes the automated functions it was programmed for, falls outside that scope. The Regulation also defines autonomous mobile machinery and the supervisor who monitors it remotely.

## Rules that never mention AI

Some of the rules that shape how AI is used in agriculture today never mention AI. Regulation 2021/2116, which governs the financing and monitoring of the EU's common agricultural policy, requires member states to have an area monitoring system working with Copernicus Sentinel satellite data or data of at least equivalent value: the system had to be operational from 1 January 2023 and fully operational in all member states by 1 January 2024 at the latest. The Regulation prescribes the satellite, not the method; it does not mention machine learning or AI. The methods that extract the crop sown from satellite imagery, and their counterpart in Türkiye, are covered in §9.

In Türkiye, the technical requirements of one of the ministry's AI projects show what is demanded while no AI-specific rule exists. The requirements for the project that will analyse fishing vessel monitoring data with AI, published in June 2026, demand that the system comply with the legislation under Law No. 6698 on the Protection of Personal Data, run in-house on a closed network, and pass a penetration test by an independent body or the ministry before going live (§14).

## Türkiye: a framework on the way, no separate annex for agriculture

As of November 2026, Türkiye has no AI-specific law. Action 4 of the Türkiye AI Action Plan (2026-2030) aims to build a regulatory environment that protects citizens' rights and gives investors predictability. Its basis is to be a national legal-ethical framework that takes account of the European Union's proportionate risk approach, with obligations graded by risk level. The targets for the first 12 months are a proportionate-risk guide with sector annexes and a National AI Ethics Board. The plan also aims to set up regulatory sandboxes in at least five priority sectors.

Which sectors will get annexes is not yet specified. In listing priority areas for the sandboxes, the plan names finance, health, energy, mobility and telecommunications in particular; agriculture is not among them. The lead institutions for the regulatory framework action are the Ministry of Industry and Technology and the Cybersecurity Directorate; the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry is not on the list of partners, which names sectoral regulators only in general terms. The Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry's own AI strategy has not yet been published (§23).
