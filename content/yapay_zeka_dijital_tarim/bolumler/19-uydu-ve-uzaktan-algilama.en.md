# 19 · Satellites and remote sensing

> **Summary.** Satellites are the widest data source for AI in agriculture, and that source being open is a policy decision: after the United States made Landsat imagery free in 2008, data use rose **20**-fold according to NASA; according to the USGS, the imagery was worth **3.45** billion dollars to its users in 2017, and if it were charged for, more than **50%** of users would be expected to decline to pay. Europe's Sentinel-2 satellites pass over the equator every **5** days at a resolution of down to **10** metres. The limit of optical satellites in agriculture is cloud: the start and middle of the season, which are critical for identifying crop types and forecasting yields, are the cloudiest period. Türkiye's domestic Earth observation satellite İMECE (**0.99** metres) lists agriculture among its uses, but there is no official channel through which its images reach agricultural institutions or farmers. The ministry says it uses satellite imagery with deep learning to detect idle farmland and orchards, but has not published an accuracy rate. There is no published assessment measuring the effect on production of AI working on satellite data in Türkiye.

## What it does

A satellite is a measuring instrument that sees fields every few days, the same way, over a wide area. The light reflected by vegetation reveals a crop's growth, water status and disease; radar satellites can also see through cloud. AI extracts from these images the crop sown, the yield, drought, and burned or idle land. Several earlier headings rest on this layer: crop monitoring and yield forecasting (§9), vessel tracking in fisheries (§14), forestry (§15), insurance (§17).

## What the field says

The US Geological Survey (USGS) made Landsat imagery free in 2008. According to a NASA page summarising a 2019 paper by Zhu and colleagues from the Landsat Science Team, data use rose **20**-fold after that decision, and scientific publications based on Landsat rose four-fold; the authors say that losing the open data policy would have a significant impact on the research community. According to the USGS's own valuation, Landsat imagery gave domestic and international users an estimated **3.45** billion dollars in benefits in 2017 (**2.19** billion in 2011); much of that value stems from the free and open data policy. Charging even small fees would lead to a loss of users and, most likely, a steep decline in the amount of imagery downloaded; more than **50%** of users are expected to decline to pay.

Sentinel-2, part of Europe's Copernicus programme, passes over the equator every **5** days with twin satellites flying in the same orbit. The multispectral optical instrument on the satellites measures in **13** bands, with a finest resolution of **10** metres; the swath is **290** kilometres wide. According to Copernicus, the satellites are used in fields such as agriculture, forestry and disaster management; Sentinel-2C was launched on 5 September 2024. The instrument is passive: it collects sunlight reflected from the Earth, which means it cannot see beneath cloud. Copernicus also runs an extension campaign that increases the revisit frequency over Europe and tropical regions of Africa and South America, which are often under heavy cloud.

Cloud is the real limit of optical satellites in agriculture. Using **10-13** years of MODIS cloud data, Whitcraft and colleagues (2015) showed that the start and middle of the growing season, which matter for identifying crop types and forecasting yields, are covered by cloud both frequently and extensively. In many important agricultural areas, less than half of the **8**-day composite images would be even **70%** clear; according to the authors, optical satellites are probably not a viable option for operational monitoring there, and alternatives such as radar should be considered. Morning acquisitions are more likely to return clear views.

## Where AI stands

Satellite data is where AI works at the widest scale in agriculture: several of the strongest results in earlier headings, such as the global canopy height map (§15) and the detection of fishing vessels invisible to public tracking (§14), were extracted from satellite imagery. That these data are open rests on a policy choice; if the choice changes, the loss of users could be large. The physical limit is cloud: optical images can be missing in exactly the weeks they are most needed.

## Türkiye

Türkiye's own Earth observation satellite İMECE is the first domestic sub-metre-resolution satellite designed and built by TÜBİTAK UZAY; it was launched on 15 April 2023. According to the institute's 2026 catalogue, its resolution is **0.99** metres in panchromatic and **3.96** metres in multispectral imaging, and its orbit is **686** kilometres; its images are used in fields such as defence, disaster management, environment and urban planning, and agriculture and forestry. The project began in 2017 and was run under the coordination of the Ministry of National Defence and the Presidency of Defence Industries; according to a pre-launch TÜBİTAK announcement, the satellite was to join the Air Force Command's inventory after in-orbit tests, with a design life of **5** years. The announcement lists agricultural applications among the areas the satellite will serve; but no official channel, and no concrete agricultural project, through which its images reach agricultural institutions or farmers has been announced. The 2027 Programme includes completing İMECE-2's integration, environmental testing and launch preparations, and İMECE-3's system integration; this work is not linked to agriculture. The minister, for his part, said in May 2025 that they would set up an agricultural observation satellite network (§23).

The ministry's 2025 activity report writes that satellite images were analysed with deep learning and AI to detect idle farmland and orchards; the report's foreword says that satellite-based remote sensing data were integrated with AI, and that crop patterns, idle land and orchards were detected precisely. The ministry has not published the accuracy of these detections or the area they cover. Under the World Bank-financed Türkiye Climate Smart and Competitive Agricultural Growth project, tender documents were prepared for a remote sensing system and a decision support system; the tender is planned to be completed in 2026. The machine learning work on crop identification (§9) and drought monitoring (§7, §17) are covered under separate headings.

Idle land is also a policy matter: the compulsory leasing by the state of land left unworked for two years in a row has begun. According to the 2027 Programme, partly as a result, owners resumed farming on **16.1** thousand of the **29.5** thousand hectares found to be idle in 2024. How this was determined has not been disclosed.

TÜBİTAK UZAY's catalogue writes that variable rate fertilisation using drone and satellite imagery in Southeastern Anatolia saved up to **25%** of fertiliser use in test fields, and that the analyses are offered to farmers and consultants in the region through a web interface. This is the institute's own claim: no method, year or sample has been published, and there is no independent assessment of it.

What is missing is **a published assessment measuring, in Türkiye, the effect of an AI application working on satellite data on production, input use or farm income; and a concrete project or data channel showing İMECE images being used in agriculture.**
