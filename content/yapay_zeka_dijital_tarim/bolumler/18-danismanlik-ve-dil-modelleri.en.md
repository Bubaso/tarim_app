# 18 · Advisory services and language models

> **Summary.** The average effect of digital farm advice has been measured, and it is modest: according to meta-analyses, information delivered by mobile phone and digital channels raises yields by **4%** and the odds of adopting recommended inputs by **22%**. Trials differ widely: in Nigeria, an app giving farmers personalised fertiliser advice raised yields by **7%** and profits by **10%**; in India, voice-based advice changed farmers' input choices but showed no systematic gain in yields or profits. The most direct evidence on language models comes from Nigeria: on **32** real farmer questions, ChatGPT's answers scored higher than those of extension agents, but fell behind on site-specific matters such as planting time and seed and fertiliser rates. In Türkiye, extension and advisory support was paid to **709** agricultural advisors and **36,831** farm holders in 2025; the ministry has no farmer-facing AI assistant, and language models have been tested on farm questions only with multiple-choice items. There is no published assessment measuring the effect of AI advice on farmers in Türkiye.

## What it does

Agricultural extension is the service that carries research knowledge to farmers: which variety to sow, when and how much fertiliser to apply, how to recognise a disease. Digital technology takes this information by text message, voice call or mobile app to farmers that extension agents cannot reach. Large language models go a step further: they understand a farmer's question in the farmer's own words and generate an answer. Their risk comes from the same place: an answer can be fluent and still not know local conditions.

## What the field says

A review by Fabregas, Kremer and Schilbach (2019) writes that the spread of mobile phones creates an opportunity to raise agricultural productivity sustainably for the **2** billion people living in smallholder farming households. According to meta-analyses, farm information delivered through digital technologies raised yields by **4%** and the odds of adopting recommended inputs by **22%**; according to the authors, the benefits likely exceed the cost of transmitting the information by an order of magnitude. But messages designed by agricultural ministries are often hard for farmers to understand and use; the authors stress that feedback mechanisms are needed so the systems can be rigorously tested and continuously improved.

Individual trials show the differences behind the average. In a randomised trial in Nigeria of a mobile app giving personalised advice on fertilising rice (Arouna and colleagues, 2021), households that received the advice raised their yields by **7%** and their profits by **10%**, without increasing their total fertiliser use. In a two-year evaluation of voice-based phone advice for cotton farmers in India (Cole and Fernando, 2021), farmers changed their sources of information and consistently adopted the recommended inputs; but no systematic gain in yields or profitability was found.

The most direct comparison for language models also comes from Nigeria (Ibrahim and colleagues, 2024). **32** questions on irrigated rice collected from farmers in Kano State were answered by six randomly selected extension agents and by ChatGPT; four evaluators scored the answers for quality and local relevance. Overall, ChatGPT's answers scored significantly higher: they received the best score nearly six times as often as the agents' (**40%** against **7%**), and the evaluators preferred ChatGPT in **78%** of cases. But ChatGPT fell behind on planting time, seed rate, and fertiliser rate and timing. According to the authors, building site-specific input rates and timing into AI assistants is critical if farmers are to use them directly.

## Where AI stands

The average effect of digital advice is small but positive and cheap; in the Nigerian trial, personalised advice delivered a gain that blanket advice had not. Language models can compete with extension agents on general farm knowledge; but they remain weak on the site- and time-specific decisions farmers actually ask about, such as when to sow and how much fertiliser to apply. The effect of advice given by a language model on farmers' yields or incomes has not yet been measured.

## Türkiye

According to the ministry's 2025 activity report, extension and advisory support payments went to **493** persons and organisations, **709** agricultural advisors and **36,831** farm holders. In farmer courses run with the Ministry of National Education, there were **1,123,252** farmer participations in **45,804** training and extension activities; the Agriculture and Forestry Academy, which runs remote training, has **100,086** registered users.

On the digital side, the ministry has **17** mobile apps. The Irrigation Management and Crop Water Use System developed by TAGEM (TAGEM-SuET) gives producers irrigation schedules in the "Su" (water) tab of the Tarım Cebimde app. The ministry says it is working on machine learning, deep learning and natural language processing as part of its AI activities; but there is no AI assistant or chatbot that farmers can use. In the 2027 Programme, the only measure for a chatbot running on AI is for the tax administration. The Sectoral Turkish Large Language Model Call, under the responsibility of the Ministry of Industry and Technology, is to encourage adapting domestic language models to sectors, but which sectors it will cover has not been announced. The Programme also aims to strengthen the link between agricultural research and extension.

In academia, language models have begun to be tested on farm questions. A study from Karamanoğlu Mehmetbey University (Eldem and Eldem, 2026) found that, on a set of **90** four-option agricultural questions, GPT-4o's average accuracy ranged from **84.8%** to **95.3%** depending on the prompting strategy, and Gemini's from **74.8%** to **88.4%**; the lowest results came when the models were asked without examples (zero-shot). The questions were compiled from three online question banks (agricultureinindia.net, sanfoundry.com, examveda.com); the authors state that they made no comparison with fine-tuning, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) or traditional methods. A study run in **96** tomato fields in Karacabey, Bursa (Şahin and colleagues, 2025) connected ChatGPT-4 to a model that recognises pests in images: the detection model reached **98.91%** precision and **98.98%** recall. But the success measured in the paper belongs to the detection model; there is no measurement of the accuracy of the explanations and recommendations given by the language model.

What is missing is **a published assessment measuring, in Türkiye, the effect of AI or language-model advice on farmers' yields, incomes or input use; or testing language models with Turkish, open-ended, site-specific farmer questions.**
