# 17 · Climate, risk and crop insurance

About **47** billion lira was transferred through insurance and support payments in Türkiye for the 2025 frost. AI can help both before and after such losses: in weather forecasting it has moved from research into operations; in insurance, models working with satellite and weather data make index insurance, which estimates losses without visiting the field, cheaper. The unsolved problem of index insurance is basis risk, the remaining risk the insurance does not cover. Türkiye has such an insurance scheme too: Village-Based Yield Insurance ties payment not to the farmer's own loss but to the village's average yield.

## What it does

In climate risk, digital technology serves three purposes: seeing the hazard coming (weather forecasts, frost and drought warnings), measuring the loss (loss assessment) and transferring the risk (insurance). AI enters the first through weather forecasting models; in the second it estimates losses from satellite imagery and weather data. Index insurance combines the two: payment is tied not to an adjuster inspecting every field but to a measurable indicator, such as an area's average yield, rainfall or crop growth tracked from satellites.

## What the field says

According to FAO's 2025 report, disasters caused an estimated **3.26** trillion dollars of losses to world agriculture between 1991 and 2023: an average of **99** billion dollars a year, roughly **4%** of global agricultural GDP. Nearly **2.9** trillion dollars of the loss came from climate-related hazards such as floods, droughts and heatwaves. FAO also writes that the sharp increase after 2010 can partly be explained by better reporting of disasters to the database, but that it also coincides with the intensification of climate-related disasters globally; an updated method led to a re-estimation of the 2023 report's figures, and the global total fell. According to FAO, AI, remote sensing, mobile connectivity, drones and sensors provide local, real-time information that improves early warning, advisory services and risk transfer; according to the Director-General's statement in the report's foreword, **9.1** million farmers access parametric insurance through digital platforms.

In weather forecasting, Lam and colleagues (2023) introduced GraphCast, a machine learning model that learns directly from past weather data. The model forecasts hundreds of weather variables for the next **10** days, globally, at **0.25**-degree resolution, in under **1** minute; it does markedly better than the most accurate operational forecasting systems on **90%** of **1,380** verification targets and also improves the prediction of severe events such as extreme temperatures. On 25 February 2025 the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) put its own AI forecasting system into operation alongside its physics-based model; according to the centre, the system outperforms physics-based models on many measures, with gains of up to **20%** on measures such as tropical cyclone tracks.

On index insurance, according to a review by Benami and colleagues (2021), loss estimation has moved from costly field sampling towards cheaper techniques based on weather and satellite data; but assessing production losses reliably and cheaply in complex landscapes remains difficult. The authors write that the value of a contract to the insured depends largely on how well it captures losses when people suffer most, and that audits are needed for inevitable misestimation. A study of livestock index insurance in northern Kenya (Jensen and colleagues, 2016) shows the problem in numbers: the insurance reduces exposure to shared risk from large losses by an average of **63%**, but policyholders are left with an average of **69%** of their original risk from large losses. According to the authors, basis risk is a widely recognised but rarely measured feature.

## Where AI stands

In weather forecasting, what is measured is the accuracy of the forecast, not the outcome in the field: these studies do not measure how much a better frost or drought forecast reduces a farmer's losses. In insurance, too, cheaper loss estimation is not enough on its own; a contract's value depends on capturing losses in the worst years, and that is still hard in complex landscapes.

## Türkiye

According to the 2027 Programme, drought and frost caused significant production losses in the 2024-2025 season. For the 2025 frost, producers with state-supported crop insurance received **23.1** billion lira in claims, and a Presidential Decision added **23.86** billion lira in support payments. In 2026 the state premium support for frost cover on crops was raised to **70%**. Crop insurance was made compulsory for products under contract farming, with an extra premium discount of **10-15%** depending on the product. In current prices, the funds for crop insurance in the agricultural support budget were **19.2** billion lira in 2025; the 2027 programme sets **30** billion lira.

The Agricultural Insurance Pool (TARSİM) issued **3.8** million policies in 2025 (**3.1** million in 2022) and paid **35** billion lira in claims to insured producers. Of the **430,076** claims under crop insurance, **171,558**, or **39.8%**, were for frost.

Village-Based Yield Insurance was launched in 2017 as district-based drought yield insurance and has run at village level since 2021; the state premium support rose from **60%** to **70%** on 1 January 2025. Cover runs up to the village's threshold yield, set using TÜİK and TARSİM data, and farmers do not need to file a claim. If the village's actual average yield falls below the threshold, all insured farmers in the village are compensated, within **30** days at the latest of the average yield being announced. If the average is equal to or above the threshold, no one is paid. Basis risk is built into this rule: if the village average does not fall below the threshold, a farmer whose own field was damaged is not paid either. No published measurement, however, shows how large this risk is.

TARSİM uses satellite data at research level. According to its 2025 activity report:

- Research was carried out on assessing damage directly from satellite images for insured parcels left unsown under village-based yield insurance.
- Flood areas were examined with Sentinel-1 radar and Sentinel-2 images, and damage assessment reports were prepared for critical locations.
- Results obtained by combining MODIS satellite data with General Directorate of Meteorology data were made applicable at village scale.

The report describes no work that uses AI or machine learning. In May 2025, however, the minister said that satellite technologies and AI would be used in the crop insurance system. There is no published study, either, measuring the effect of AI on loss assessment, crop insurance or frost and drought warnings in Türkiye.

In early warning, the General Directorate of Meteorology's Agricultural Frost Warning System provides **5**-day frost forecasts and risk levels by crop for every province and district; frost risk maps for the next **5** days are published online every day, and the Agricultural Weather Forecast mobile app brings severe weather warnings to farmers. Meteorological drought is analysed with internationally accepted indices (SPI, PNI, PDSI). The directorate says in its 2026 performance programme that it has used AI-supported forecasting systems since 2025; it has not disclosed which forecasts use them or how accurate they are. The Agricultural Drought Monitoring System (TAKİS) tracks drought with satellite data at seven-day intervals; work on forecasting drought **1-4** weeks ahead with AI is planned to begin in 2027. The Programme also foresees a national flood and drought forecasting and early warning system in all river basins.
