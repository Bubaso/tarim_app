# 17 · Climate, risk and crop insurance

> **Summary.** In weather forecasting AI has moved from research into operations: GraphCast outperforms the most accurate operational forecasting systems on **90%** of **1,380** verification targets in **10**-day global forecasts, and Europe's medium-range weather forecasting centre has run its own AI model alongside its physics-based model since February 2025. How much a better forecast reduces losses in the field, however, has not been measured. In insurance, satellite and weather data make index insurance, which estimates losses without visiting the field, cheaper; the unsolved problem is basis risk: in Kenya, households with index insurance still carry an average of **69%** of their risk from large losses. Türkiye has a working index insurance: Village-Based Yield Insurance ties payment not to the farmer's own loss but to the village's average yield, and the state pays **70%** of the premium. About **47** billion lira was transferred through insurance and support payments for the 2025 frost. TARSİM still uses satellites only at research level; neither its 2025 activity report nor the General Directorate of Meteorology's performance programme gives AI a role. There is no published assessment measuring the effect of AI on early warning, loss assessment or insurance in Türkiye.

## What it does

In climate risk, digital technology serves three purposes: seeing the hazard coming (weather forecasts, frost and drought warnings), measuring the loss (loss assessment) and transferring the risk (insurance). AI enters the first through weather forecasting models; in the second it estimates losses from satellite imagery and weather data. Index insurance combines the two: payment is tied not to an adjuster inspecting every field but to a measurable indicator, such as an area's average yield, rainfall or crop growth tracked from satellites.

## What the field says

According to FAO's 2025 report, disasters caused an estimated **3.26** trillion dollars of losses to world agriculture between 1991 and 2023: an average of **99** billion dollars a year, roughly **4%** of global agricultural output. Nearly **2.9** trillion dollars of the loss came from climate-related hazards such as floods, droughts and heatwaves. FAO also writes that the sharp increase after 2010 can partly be explained by better reporting of disasters to the database; an updated method led to a re-estimation of the 2023 report's figures, and the global total fell. According to FAO, AI, remote sensing, mobile connectivity, drones and sensors provide local, real-time information that improves early warning, advisory services and risk transfer; according to the Director-General's statement in the report's foreword, **9.1** million farmers access parametric insurance through digital platforms.

In weather forecasting, Lam and colleagues (2023) introduced GraphCast, a machine learning model that learns directly from past weather data. The model forecasts hundreds of weather variables for the next **10** days, globally, at **0.25**-degree resolution, in under **1** minute; it does markedly better than the most accurate operational forecasting systems on **90%** of **1,380** verification targets and also improves the prediction of severe events such as extreme temperatures. On 25 February 2025 the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) put its own AI forecasting system into operation alongside its physics-based model; according to the centre, the system outperforms physics-based models on many measures, with gains of up to **20%** on measures such as tropical cyclone tracks.

On index insurance, according to a review by Benami and colleagues (2021), loss estimation has moved from costly field sampling towards cheaper techniques based on weather and satellite data; but assessing production losses reliably and cheaply in complex landscapes remains difficult. The authors write that the value of a contract to the insured depends largely on how well it captures losses when people suffer most, and that audits are needed for inevitable misestimation. A study of livestock index insurance in northern Kenya (Jensen and colleagues, 2016) shows the problem in numbers: the insurance reduces exposure to shared risk from large losses by an average of **63%**, but policyholders are left with an average of **69%** of their original risk from large losses. According to the authors, basis risk, the remaining risk the insurance does not cover, is a widely recognised but rarely measured feature.

## Where AI stands

In weather forecasting AI has moved from research into operations; but what is measured is the accuracy of the forecast, not the outcome in the field. How much a better frost or drought forecast reduces a farmer's losses has not yet been measured. In insurance, satellites and models make loss estimation cheaper; but a contract's value depends on capturing losses in the worst years, and that is still hard in complex landscapes.

## Türkiye

According to the 2027 Programme, drought and frost caused significant production losses in the 2024-2025 season. For the 2025 frost, producers with state-supported crop insurance received **23.1** billion lira in claims, and a Presidential Decision added **23.86** billion lira in support payments: about **47** billion lira in total. In 2026 the state premium support for frost cover on crops was raised to **70%**. Crop insurance was made compulsory for products under contract farming, with an extra premium discount of **10-15%** depending on the product. In current prices, the funds for crop insurance in the agricultural support budget were **19.2** billion lira in 2025; the 2027 programme sets **30** billion lira.

The Agricultural Insurance Pool (TARSİM) issued **3.8** million policies in 2025 (**3.1** million in 2022) and paid **35** billion lira in claims to insured producers. Of the **430,076** claims under crop insurance, **171,558**, or **39.8%**, were for frost.

Türkiye also has an index insurance. Launched in 2017 as district-based drought yield insurance, it has run at village level since 2021; the state premium support rose from **60%** to **70%** on 1 January 2025. In Village-Based Yield Insurance, cover runs up to the village's threshold yield, set using TÜİK and TARSİM data, and farmers do not need to file a claim. If the village's actual average yield falls below the threshold, all insured farmers in the village are compensated, within **30** days at the latest of the average yield being announced. If the average is equal to or above the threshold, no one is paid. Basis risk is built into this rule: if the village average does not fall below the threshold, a farmer whose own field was damaged is not paid either.

TARSİM uses satellite data at research level. According to its 2025 activity report, research was carried out on assessing damage directly from satellite images for insured parcels left unsown under village-based yield insurance; flood areas were examined with Sentinel-1 radar and Sentinel-2 images, and damage assessment reports were prepared for critical locations; results obtained by combining MODIS satellite data with General Directorate of Meteorology data were made applicable at village scale. The report gives AI and machine learning no role. The minister, for his part, said in May 2025 that they would use satellite technologies and AI in the crop insurance system (§23).

In early warning, the General Directorate of Meteorology's Agricultural Frost Warning System provides **5**-day frost forecasts and risk levels by crop for every province and district; frost risk maps for the next **5** days are published online every day, and the Agricultural Weather Forecast mobile app brings severe weather warnings to farmers. Meteorological drought is analysed with internationally accepted indices (SPI, PNI, PDSI). The directorate's 2025 performance programme gives AI no role. The ministry's Agricultural Drought Monitoring System (TAKİS) tracks drought with satellite data at seven-day intervals; work on forecasting drought **1-4** weeks ahead with AI is planned to begin in 2027 (§7, §23). The Programme also foresees a national flood and drought forecasting and early warning system in all river basins.

What is missing is **a published assessment measuring, in Türkiye, the effect of AI on frost and drought warnings, loss assessment or crop insurance; or measuring the basis risk of Village-Based Yield Insurance.**
