# 14 · Fisheries and aquaculture

> **Summary.** In 2022 aquaculture overtook capture fisheries in aquatic animal production for the first time in history (**51%**); meanwhile, the share of marine stocks fished within biologically sustainable levels fell to **62.3%**. The most striking AI result this dossier found comes from catch monitoring: a map built from satellite imagery and deep learning revealed that **72-76%** of the world's industrial fishing vessels are not visible in public tracking systems. In aquaculture, deep learning is being tried for fish recognition, feeding and biomass estimation, but its need for labelled data limits its spread. In Türkiye, data from **1,629** fishing vessels of **12** metres and over are collected via satellite and GSM; for now these data are used mainly for monitoring and record keeping, and the technical requirements document for a ministry project to analyse them with AI was published in 2026.

## What it does

In fisheries and aquaculture, digital technology works in two different worlds. In aquaculture, cameras and sensors in cages and ponds monitor the fish, the feed and the water: fish numbers and size, feeding behaviour, water temperature and oxygen. In capture fisheries the task is to track the vessel: position transmitters, electronic logbooks and satellite vessel detection show which vessel fished where, for how long and what it caught.

In the first world, AI recognises fish in images and tries to estimate feeding decisions and biomass; in the second, it extracts fishing behaviour from position tracks and finds the vessels that fall outside tracking.

## What the field says

According to FAO's 2024 report, world fisheries and aquaculture production reached a record **223.2** million tonnes in 2022. In aquatic animals, aquaculture reached **94.4** million tonnes, **51%** of the total, overtaking capture fisheries for the first time in history. On the capture side the picture is worsening: the share of marine stocks fished within biologically sustainable levels fell to **62.3%** in 2021.

Paolo and colleagues (2024) combined satellite imagery, vessel GPS data and deep-learning models to map industrial vessel activity in the world's coastal waters in 2017-2021. The result: **72-76%** of industrial fishing vessels are not visible in public tracking systems, with much of this fishing around South Asia, Southeast Asia and Africa. For transport and energy vessels, the activity missing from tracking is **21-30%**. Fishing fell by **12%** at the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic and had not returned to pre-pandemic levels by 2021.

## Where AI stands

In aquaculture, deep learning is being tried for live fish recognition, species classification, behavioural analysis, feeding decisions, size and biomass estimation, and water-quality prediction. According to the review by Yang and colleagues (2021), its greatest contribution is its ability to extract features from the data automatically. But the method is still at a weak artificial intelligence stage and needs large amounts of labelled data for training; according to the review, this need is the bottleneck holding back its spread in aquaculture.

In catch monitoring, by contrast, AI does something at a scale no human could watch by hand: it makes visible the vessels that fall outside public tracking.

## Türkiye

According to the ministry's data, Türkiye produced **1,037,019** tonnes of fish and seafood in 2025: **410,428** tonnes from capture and **626,591** tonnes from aquaculture. **70.4%** of aquaculture is at sea. Trout leads in inland waters (**185,310** tonnes), sea bass (**174,859** tonnes) and sea bream (**164,643** tonnes) at sea; the most-caught fish was anchovy (**245,261** tonnes). According to the 2027 Programme, **2,502** aquaculture facilities with a capacity of **925** thousand tonnes a year are in operation; **17** species in capture and aquaculture have been brought into production planning, covering **80%** of production.

For monitoring catches, Türkiye has a digital infrastructure. The Fishing Vessel Monitoring System (BAGİS) tracks the position, route and speed of commercial fishing vessels of **12** metres and over in real time via satellite and GSM; vessels send a position about every **10** minutes. The Fisheries Information System (SUBİS) records the species and quantity caught through electronic logbooks and transport documents. According to the ministry's technical requirements document, published on TÜBİTAK's website in June 2026, data for **1,629** vessels are current and accessible; but this data pool is for now used mainly for monitoring and record keeping, and checks such as entry into prohibited areas are done by staff at screens. The document also notes that data gaps can occur because of technical faults or fishers tampering with the device. In the project it describes, BAGİS data will be analysed with AI to detect possible violations in prohibited areas and generate warnings.

According to an announcement of April 2026, BAGİS devices on vessels of 12 metres and over are to be replaced; in the first phase, new devices will be fitted to vessels of **19** metres and over, with installation planned to finish by the end of 2026.

In academia, machine learning has reached the feeding side of aquaculture: a study involving Muğla Sıtkı Koçman University used machine learning to examine how **11** physical and chemical characteristics of water relate to fish feed consumption; temperature emerged as the most important variable (Özdemir and colleagues, 2025).

What this dossier's scan did not find is **a published assessment measuring, on a farm or in a fleet in Türkiye, the effect of AI on feeding, fish health or catch-monitoring outcomes.**
