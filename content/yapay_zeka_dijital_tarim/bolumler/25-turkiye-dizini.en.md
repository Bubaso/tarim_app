# 25 · Türkiye directory: start-ups, universities, public projects

In Türkiye, work on AI and digital technology in agriculture is under way in four places: in the systems of public bodies, in the state's research and support programmes, in universities and in start-ups. In the public sector, AI is mostly used, or being prepared for use, in monitoring: identifying crops, idle land and orchards from satellites, decision support in forest fires, tracking fishing vessels. In the **10** projects accepted in smart agriculture, food and livestock under TÜBİTAK's 1711 AI Ecosystem Call, companies work together with universities and research institutes. On the start-up side, a 2025 report by FAO and the EBRD counts about **35** agricultural technology start-ups in Türkiye and finds this number relatively low for the size of the country and its farm sector.

## Public sector: registration, monitoring and warning systems

These systems are the digital record-keeping and measurement layer of agriculture: they hold the data a model learns from, the measurements a forecast relies on and the channel through which a warning reaches the farmer.

| System | What it does |
|---|---|
| Farmer Registration System (ÇKS) · Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry | Registers the land used for crop production, the producers and their production |
| Wholesale Market Registration System (HKS) | A central, internet-based register of fruit and vegetable trade |
| B-Reçete System | Brings the prescription, sale, application and recording of plant protection products together electronically; in all **81** provinces since 1 July 2026 |
| TAGEM-SuET · TAGEM | Irrigation management and crop water use; on the web and in the Water tab of the Tarım Cebimde app |
| Digital Irrigation Management System (DİSU) · Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry | To be completed in 2027, according to the 2027 Annual Programme |
| Agricultural Frost Warning System (ZDUS) · General Directorate of Meteorology | **5**-day frost forecasts and risk status by crop for every province and district |
| Village-Based Yield Insurance · TARSİM | Started in 2017 as district-based drought yield insurance and has been village-based since 2021; in 2025 TARSİM carried out research on damage assessment with satellite images |
| İMECE · TÜBİTAK UZAY | Türkiye's first domestically built sub-metre-resolution Earth observation satellite; launched on 15 April 2023; development of İMECE-2 and İMECE-3 is under way |

## Research and support programmes

**TÜBİTAK's 1711 AI Ecosystem Call** aims to turn AI technologies into products or solutions that meet the needs of client organisations. The first call opened in 2022 and a call opens every year; one of the programme's five priority areas is smart agriculture, food and livestock. So far the programme has supported **64** projects with **325,089,753** lira; this total covers all five areas. According to TÜBİTAK BİLGEM's list, the projects accepted in smart agriculture, food and livestock are:

| Project | Topic | Technology provider or stakeholder | University or research institute |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yıldırım Plastik | An AI-based sorting machine that separates single-plant pollen from mixed pollen | Vitex Honey (stakeholder) | Ankara University |
| Koluman | A system that detects animals' oestrus behaviour from images; herd management | Eltassan Elektronik | Çukurova University |
| Uzbaş | A precision farming system that detects downy mildew, grape berry moth and nutrient deficiency in vineyards with drones and AI, and sprays | MET İleri Teknoloji | Ege University, İzmir Institute of Technology |
| Ülker Çikolata | Designing functional foods with AI | Dynamis (stakeholder) | Istanbul Technical University |
| Ak Gıda | Forecasting raw milk supply and price; simulating farm profitability | Medyasoft | Yıldız Technical University |
| Doğanlar | A precision farming system that analyses soil with sensors fitted to tillage equipment (NİTAS) | Gökdoğan Teknoloji | Marmara, Karamanoğlu Mehmetbey and Ankara universities |
| Merlab | An AI-based herd management system | Gençtar Makine | TAGEM |
| Protel Bilgisayar | An autonomous robot that fertilises, sprays and harvests in a tomato greenhouse | Sistematik Atölye | Bursa Uludağ University, TAGEM |
| Easyflex | An adaptive, AI-assisted silicone gripper for fruit-harvesting robots | Filossoft | Ankara University |
| Camandıra | An AI-based smart irrigation system driven by sensor data | Görsentam | Bursa Technical University |

These are projects that were found worthy of support. According to the Plant Protection Central Research Institute, one of its partners, the greenhouse robot project started on 1 January 2026.

**TÜBİTAK BİLGEM's AI Institute** has a Smart Agriculture, Food and Livestock competence centre. Its fields of work include estimating production from satellite images and soil analysis results, and raising meat and milk output with continuous measurements taken from animals. The centre's page also mentions cooperation with the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry's general directorates of Food and Control and of Livestock.

**The Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry**, according to the minister's statement of 4 June 2026, has set up an Artificial Intelligence and Digital Agriculture Technologies Research Centre.

**The Türkiye Climate Smart and Competitive Agricultural Growth Project (TUCSAP)** is financed by the World Bank; according to the ministry's 2025 activity report, grant programmes under the project had not yet started.

**The Digital Villages Initiative** is a technical cooperation project of TAGEM and FAO: it started in 2023, was completed in December 2025 and covered Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Georgia and Türkiye.

**TEKNOFEST's Agricultural Technologies Competition** has awarded places every year since 2020; high school and university students, graduates, start-ups and private companies can take part. The Ankara Development Agency's **TAGTech Programme**, according to the FAO–EBRD report, provides farmers and agricultural technology start-ups with field integration and R&D support.

## Start-ups

The start-ups in the FAO–EBRD report work in smart and precision agriculture and related fields such as supply chain management and agricultural finance. The report's list of start-ups was taken from the 2022 Türkiye Agriculture Ecosystem map and completed with web research and meetings with about half of the start-ups; there is no regularly updated list of agricultural technology start-ups in Türkiye. FAO and the EBRD state that they do not endorse the companies named in the report. For the start-ups in the report that present an agricultural technology service on their own websites, the founding year and city come from the report; the descriptions of what they do are the companies' own statements:

The report lists Actimoo and BenimSürüm among the start-ups set up as subsidiaries of established companies. According to the report, Ankaref began R&D on a low-cost oestrus detection system for dairy cows with Ankara University and Algan Group in 2016 and launched a pedometer under the Actimoo brand in 2020; BenimSürüm's parent company is Algan Group. AgCurate was founded in Ankara; the address the company gives on its website is now in the Netherlands. Görsentam is the technology provider of the smart irrigation project in TÜBİTAK 1711.
