Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) show superior performance in different fields. However, the applications of these models, which have shown superior performance in many studies, are still limited and incomplete in agriculture. In this study, a comprehensive evaluation has been made the use of LLMs in the field of agriculture. A set of multiple-choice questions was developed, covering three topics (General, Horticulture, Crop Production) and three difficulty levels (Easy, Medium, Difficult). For each question, answers were generated using GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash LLMs. Zero-Shot, Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Self-Consistency, and Tree-of-Thought (ToT) techniques were preferred as prompt strategies. An automatically optimized prompting pipeline was also applied using Automatic Prompt Engineering (APE) to improve reasoning ability in agricultural question answering. Furthermore, the effect of the prompt methods used on both the accuracy and consistency of the answers was examined. In this study applied in the field of agriculture, the general success of question-answering systems (QAs) was evaluated and the effect of optimized prompts on system success was examined. The findings revealed that LLMs were generally successful, but their results varied significantly depending on the preferred LLM and prompt strategy. The results obtained were analyzed in detail statistically using bootstrap confidence intervals, paired t-tests, ANOVA, and effect size measures (Cohen’s h and d) within the scope of the LLM model, prompt method, difficulty levels, and category-based format. This study introduces one of the first domain-specific question answering systems powered by LLMs, tailored for agricultural experts such as engineers and technicians, and presents an innovative approach by creating an infrastructure for smart digital applications in the field of agriculture.

Introduction

Increasing applications in artificial intelligence and natural language processing have enabled the use of LLMs in many areas. When recent studies are examined, it is seen that pre-trained LLMs have demonstrated remarkable success, especially when examined in terms of decision making and language1. In recent years, LLMs have been used in many fields such as education2, health3,4, industry, agriculture5,6,7, basic sciences8, political science9, and have become one of the important milestones in human-computer interaction in these fields10. Basically, LLMs are trained using large-scale language sets such as websites11, online resources, books, articles12. LLMs trained with large datasets show human-like performance in many tasks such as answering questions, generating content, and interpreting13,14,15,16.

In recent years, developments in the fields of information and communication technologies, data analysis, automation systems, smart agriculture, and digital agriculture have caused a significant increase in the work done in the field of agriculture17. In particular, it is aimed to minimize environmental impacts and provide maximum efficiency with sustainable and sensitive agricultural practices18, which ensures that artificial intelligence applications used in agriculture are increasing day by day19. However, if existing data can be processed properly with LLMs, critical processes such as decision making in agricultural practices and increasing sustainability will become more meaningful20,21.

In this context, LLMs are used for purposes such as providing documentation in agricultural practices, consultancy services, training, producing real-time solutions to encountered problems, managing the production processes of products17, and especially farmer support services22. One of the most important points here is asking the right question in order to obtain the correct answer from LLMs. Many prompt strategies have been developed to improve the performance of LLMs and to avoid situations such as LLMs producing answers as if they were correct or producing answers in the wrong format. In particular, this increases the importance of using intelligent chatbots in question and answer systems23.

Prompt engineering plays an important role24 in many stages such as asking the right and effective questions25, making questions clearer by rephrasing them, and providing specific task definitions when answering questions, thus maximizing the potential of artificial intelligence models26,27. A well-written prompt will provide more successful results as it will enable a LLM like ChatGPT to be used more correctly. Many academic studies have started to use prompting methods for LLMs28,29,30,31,32.

By systematically comparing prompt strategies across various topics and difficulty levels in agriculture, this research provides new insights into prompt optimization for domain-specific applications.

In Web of Science, SCI-E, SSCI, and ESCI indexed publication, shown in Fig. 1, large language model and agriculture as topic keyword, the increase in publications, especially in recent years, stands out. The figure summarizes the increase in the number of studies conducted on LLMs, especially in the field of agriculture, and the increase in the number of citations over the last 20 years. There is a serious gap in the use of LLMs, particularly in agriculture. Unlike existing studies that focus on general-purpose LLMs, a domain-specific evaluation is provided framework targeting agricultural professionals.

The agricultural sector is an area where decisions taken vary depending on regional factors and are of great importance, especially in the field of production. In addition, experts in the field of agriculture such as agricultural engineers and agricultural technicians may have difficulty in accessing information accurately and quickly. When these situations are evaluated, the need for an LLMs-based question and answer system is increasing day by day.

Within the scope of this study, an LLMs-based Agricultural Question-Answering system (AgriQAs) has been created that can be used by experts in the field of agriculture such as agricultural engineers and agricultural technicians. The dataset created for the proposed system shown in Fig. 2 includes multiple choice questions and answers. For each question in the generated dataset, answers were generated using GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash LLMs. The generated answers were compared with the answers stored in the system and the results were evaluated.

The research objectives and contributions of this study are summarized below:

  • An LLM-based QA system called AgriQAs was created for experts working professionally in the field of agriculture.

  • Different prompt techniques were used to answer the questions in the created question-answer dataset with ChatGPT and Gemini LLMs. In order for the LLMs preferred in the proposed system to produce more accurate and consistent answers, the prompts of the Zero-Shot, Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Self-Consistency, and Tree of Thought (ToT) methods were optimized with Automatic Prompt Engineer (APE).

  • The success and error rates of the LLMs and prompt techniques used in the system are evaluated and the factors affecting the performance of these techniques are analyzed and explained in detail.

  • Statistical analyses including bootstrap confidence intervals, paired t-tests, ANOVA, and effect size measures (Cohen’s h and d) were conducted to evaluate performance differences in the LLM model, prompt method, difficulty levels, and category-based format.

  • The use of LLMs with different prompt techniques in knowledge-based questions in the field of agriculture is intended to fill the gap in this field and to lead the proposed system to experts in this sector.

The innovative approaches in this study can be summarized as follows:

  • Within the scope of the research, an innovative approach that optimizes LLMs, unlike traditional methods, has been developed. This innovative approach has been presented as a digital assistant infrastructure applicable to the agricultural sector.

  • In the proposed system, better performance is achieved compared to existing approaches by strengthening the reasoning ability of ChatGPT with good prompt guidance.

  • An innovative approach has been presented that will form the basis for smart digital agricultural applications.

  • LLM models have been optimized using advanced system engineering techniques for question-answer systems in the field of agriculture.

Literature review

In this section, information is provided by examining the studies of LLMs in the agricultural field. In addition, the advantages and disadvantages of LLMs with traditional methods are mentioned.

Zhao et al., proposed a ChatGPT based method to classify agricultural text data. They used different prompt techniques and compared the results with machine learning, word embedding, PLM-based fine tuning methods5. Stella et al. have shown that LLM, like ChatGPT, can be used in robot design processes, both conceptually and technically33. Chen et al., conducted a study on the use of question and answer systems in the agricultural field with ChatGPT. They proposed a Bert-based approach to obtain meaningful inference from questions in the agricultural field34. De et al., proposed an encoder-based model for Named Entity Recognition (NER) and Relation Extraction (RE) tasks in the agricultural field. The results obtained were compared with SciBERT, SpanBERT and ChatGPT. In the application they developed, they achieved 98% success for NER and 97% for RE20. Ali et al., developed an AI-based chatbot by preparing a set of 32 questions related to irrigated rice cultivation. It was observed that the answers received from ChatGPT were more successful when the questions were answered by both ChatGPT and extension agents35. In another study, an application with a QAs was developed in the field of agriculture36,37 evaluated the answers given by ChatGPT with different questions they created to measure the effectiveness of ChatGPT in the field of agriculture and animal husbandry. Kuska et al., examined the technical applications of LLMs in crop production and evaluated how LLMs can benefit in areas such as decision support and training12. Benos et al. used the O*NET database in their study to evaluate the sensitivity of LLMs, including ChatGPT, Copilot, and Gemini, to robotization of agricultural occupations. They compared the values ​​obtained from the LLMs with expert opinions19. Within the scope of agricultural applications, Reinforcement learning (RL) and LLMs have been integrated into the crop production process38. Jin et al., conducted a study examining both the QAs and inter-answer alignment techniques of ChatGPT39. Tian et al., have created a detailed review article examining studies involving QAs in smart agriculture applications23.

Research gap

To summarize, although applications such as machine learning and deep learning in the field of agriculture have increased in recent years, these models have some disadvantages36. These disadvantages include large datasets, the need for machines with high processing power to process the data, high training times, and the need to include an expert in the system. However, recent studies have shown that LLM-based applications exhibit significant success, especially when examined in terms of decision making and language. Therefore, LLMs were preferred in this study.

Compared to traditional models, LLMs can produce meaningful outputs with very little labeled data and do not require domain-specific model training. This feature offers a significant advantage in fields like agriculture, where labeled datasets are scarce or costly to produce. Furthermore, thanks to effective prompt engineering techniques, LLMs can simulate domain knowledge without requiring direct expert integration into the system. Despite the increasing use of LLMs in fields such as education, healthcare, and law, studies systematically evaluating these models in structured question-answer systems specifically for agriculture are extremely limited. This study aims to fill this important gap by comparatively examining different prompt strategies.

Furthermore, existing studies in agricultural AI often lack rigorous statistical assessments such as confidence intervals, effect size measures, and formal significance tests, limiting the reliability and reproducibility of findings. Most comparative LLM studies rely primarily on descriptive statistics rather than comprehensive inferential analysis to verify performance differences.

Materials and methods

In this study, a LLMs-based system design was developed by creating a question-answer set. The general structure of the system, which was designed to produce the correct answers to the given questions, is given in Fig. 3.

As seen in the Fig. 3;

  • Step1: At this stage, a set of questions related to the field of agriculture was created. There are 4 options for each question and the answer to each question is also available in the system. The question-answer set was saved as.csv extension.

  • Step2: Prompts were generated with Zero-Shot, CoT, ToT, and Self-Consistency methods to be used in GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash. 8 different approaches were generated with 2 different LLMs and 4 different prompt methods. Each of the questions in the question set was evaluated separately with Zero-Shot, CoT, ToT, and Self-Consistency prompt methods and the system was enabled to choose one of the answers according to the specified answers. Then, in order to obtain more correct answers and increase the success rate, the prompts generated for Zero-Shot, CoT, ToT, and Self-Consistency were optimized with APE. The newly optimized prompts were run 5 times on each question again.

  • Step3: At the end of each run in Step2, the answer to the multiple-choice question obtained from GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash was saved in a csv file as a, b, c, and d with the method name.

  • Step4: At this stage, the answer value obtained from each approach was compared with the real answer value. The general success and error rates of each approach were examined. The results were evaluated separately according to the prompting strategy used, the types of questions and the difficulty level of the questions.

In this section, the dataset collected in the creation of the question-answer infrastructure in the field of agriculture (Section Data Preparation) and the methods used to create consistent and accurate answers in the proposed system (Section Methods) are mentioned.

Data Preparation

Question-Answering systems(OAs) are systems that consist of large data sets and aim to produce correct answers to questions given as input40. However, the creation of large data sets related to the subject is quite costly in terms of both time and money. For this reason, chatbots are preferred41 to produce correct answers to the given input questions. In this study, GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash were used as chatbots.

Within the scope of this study, the question set named AgriQAs created about agriculture consists of 90 multiple choice questions and answers in total. In order to increase the reliability of the created dataset, questions and answers with verified answers in sources42,43,44 were preferred. The infrastructure of the AgriQAs dataset is illustrated in Fig. 4. In the content of the created data set, an id definition was made for each question and fields such as question, answer options, answer, difficulty level, and question group were added. As seen in Fig. 4, each of the question groups prepared on the topics of General, Horticulture, and Crop Production has 30 different questions and 4-option answers. In addition, the questions were classified as easy, medium and difficult according to their difficulty level and a question pool was created with 30 questions from each level. The distribution of the created question set is given in detail in Table 1. The equal distribution of questions at each difficulty level and category ensured a balanced evaluation of performance during the comparison of LLM models and prompt methods. While determining the levels of the questions: (i) questions containing a minimum level of technical terms were labeled as easy, (ii) questions requiring a medium level of conceptual knowledge and interpretation related to agriculture were labeled as medium, (iii) questions requiring high-level knowledge and multi-step inference were labeled as difficult. Defining these topics and difficulty levels at the outset allows for the creation and use of measurable criteria. This ensures the objectivity and reproducibility of the dataset. It also allows other researchers to conduct different studies using the same criteria.

Methods

ChatGPT is a chatbot developed by OpenAI45,46 based on the Generative Pre-trained Transformer (GPT) architecture47, and Gemini is another chatbot developed by Google AI48. Both are artificial intelligence-based chatbots and are used in many areas such as health, agriculture, education, journalism, editorial support, and text production37.They also have the ability to produce human-like texts by being trained with large-scale text data. In addition, they exhibit high performance in many areas such as successfully continuing complex tasks, successfully continuing dialogues, and presenting detailed outputs to users49,50. In this study, GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash, which support question and answer systems by interacting with users, were preferred.

In addition, GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash were chosen due to their robust multilingual support, efficient processing capabilities, and successful responses to agricultural terminology. The domain-specific reasoning capabilities of both models allow for more accurate and consistent evaluation of complex agricultural queries. Furthermore, the robust API support of both models, combined with their comprehensive documentation infrastructures, allowed for the evaluation of the study using different prompting techniques. Finally, the different developers of these two LLM models (OpenAI and Google) are preferred in this study, as they are in many academic studies for comparative analyses of different architectures.

In addition, different prompt methods have been developed to increase the performance of LLMs51 such as ChatGPT and Gemini, to make decisions and to provide accurate and consistent answers. In this study, Zero-Shot, CoT, Self-Consistency, and ToT methods, schematically shown in Fig. 5, were preferred.

  • Zero-Shot: In this method, the LLM is expected to give the answer directly52. It does not need any interpretation text. It is faster than other methods and works with short prompts. It is also seen that very successful results are obtained in reasoning problems53.

  • Chain-of-Thought (CoT): The LLM that is created produces the answer for the given text by specifying the thinking steps. In this way, it is possible to examine step by step how the relevant answer is reached. The accuracy of the model is determined by comparing the created intermediary texts with the real text54,55.

  • Self-Consistency: While creating an answer related to the text given to the created LLM, more than one answer is created56 and the consistency of the obtained answers with the main text is checked. The answers created with various logical methods are analyzed and the most consistent answer is preferred57,58.

  • Tree-of-Thought (ToT): In this method, the LLM that is created divides the given text into sub-parts and produces a series of text fragments as a continuation of the text59. In this way, a chain of thought is formed and a consistent connection is created between the main text and the sub-texts60,61 and it uses the thought tree method to explain the process steps24.

  • Automatic Prompt Engineer (APE): It provides automatic creation of prompts to be used in the created model62. At this point, a trigger sentence is needed when creating the prompt. The fact that automatically created prompts are as good as prompts created by humans63 allows problems to be solved without the need for human intervention in this method.

Prompt design was made so that only 1 answer would be produced by each model. At the same time, the APE method was used to strengthen and optimize prompts. In other words, the APE method was applied to the textual structure of the prompts. This process focused on improving the structure of the instruction and role components to ensure more accurate and consistent responses. The questions and answers were preserved without any changes during this process. In this way, the proposed system was able to provide more accurate answers by reducing the need for human intervention. The representations of the created and optimized prompts for each prompt model are given in Fig. 6.

For example, when examining the Zero-Shot method, an agricultural science expert was asked to choose one of four answers, while a more specific task definition was provided by assigning a professional role in the APE-optimized prompt. This enabled a more expert role to be used in the evaluation of the model, resulting in more accurate and consistent responses in the agricultural field.

When the CoT prompt was evaluated, the agricultural science expert was asked to develop a step-by-step thought process and a response was requested. In the APE-optimized prompt, a professional role was assigned and a detailed task description was provided. This detailed task description included: summary, identify the topic, break down the question into parts, eliminate incorrect answers using detailed reasoning, and justify the final choice. This structure demonstrates that APE, through detailed analysis, generates a chain of thought and enables internal evaluation before delivering the final answer.

When evaluating the Self-Consistency prompt, an agricultural science expert was asked to evaluate questions and answers in the agricultural field and provide answers based on four options using clear reasoning. The APE-optimized prompt assigned a professional role, enabling step-by-step analysis to ensure the most logically consistent answer. This allowed the APE to provide the most consistent answer using alternative answers, making the response more reliable. It also appears to enhance internal reasoning.

When evaluating the ToT prompt, an agricultural science expert was asked to evaluate questions and answers in the agricultural field by applying at least a few reasoning strategies while thinking. In the APE-optimized prompt, a professional role was assigned and asked to create a thinking system based on at least three different strategies: factual recall, elimination, and contextual application. The APE-generated prompt allows for the discovery of different systematic ways of thinking to help make the most accurate decisions.

Experimental results

Within the scope of this study, GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash LLMs were used to answer the questions with 4 different prompt techniques for the AgriQAs multiple choice question set. In this section, the accuracy and error rates used to find the correct answers for each approach created, and the error rates in each group when the AgriQAs questions were evaluated according to their difficulty levels and question categories were examined in detail.

Accuracy

In questions prepared about agriculture, the accuracy results obtained when GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash, were run with different prompt methods are shown in Fig. 7, and the average success rates and standard deviation values ​​obtained are shown in detail in Table 2. As shown in Fig. 7 and Table 2, it is seen that the success of the proposed method in selecting the correct option is over 74% for all models.

However, each approach was run 5 times each. When GPT-4o (blue color in Fig. 7) and Gemini-2.0-flash (orange color in Fig. 7) are evaluated in general, it is seen that GPT-4o is more successful. The percentage accuracy score and standard deviation values (in parentheses) in Table 2 were calculated by averaging the values obtained from 5 runs for each approach. Figure 7 shows the accuracy score values obtained from each experiment individually.

The average success rate for prompts run with GPT-4o ranged from 84.8% to 95.3%. The highest value of 95.3% was obtained from GPT-4o with Self-Consistency. The accuracy values ​​for prompts run with Gemini-2.0-flash ranged from 74.8% to 88.4%. The results obtained with Gemini-2.0-flash with ToT reached the highest value of 88.4%.

In both GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash, the lowest performance was obtained with Zero-Shot. The working logic of Zero-Shot is a question and answer relationship. That is, there are no processing steps such as generating intermediate values, reasoning or directing the model’s problem solving process. The low accuracy value in the question set created especially in a specific field such as agriculture shows that Zero-Shot’s ability to solve questions in specific fields is also weak. All of these reasons justify the low results obtained from Zero-Shot.

When GPT-4o with Self-Consistency and GPT-4o with ToT were evaluated, GPT-4o achieved over 91% success in both prompt models. This is because both prompt models have advanced strategies. Since reasoning-supported prompts are created in both, their success rates are high. The same situation is valid for Gemini-2.0-flash with Self-Consistency and Gemini-2.0-flash with ToT, and the accuracy rate of both models has the highest value.

The difference between GPT-4o with Self-Consistency and GPT-4o with Zero-Shot is 10.5%, and the difference between Gemini-2.0-flash with ToT and Gemini-2.0-flash with Zero-Shot is 13.6%, which shows how important the prompt selection method is, especially when using LLMs.

For both GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash, answering all questions five times made it possible to observe the consistencies in both LLMs more clearly.

It is appropriate to evaluate the reliability of approaches not only by looking at their accuracy values ​​but also by their standard deviation values. When the values ​​given in Table 2 are examined, it is seen that GPT-4o with Self-Consistency gives more consistent and correct answers with the highest accuracy, and that this situation is confirmed by the standard deviation value and reliable results are obtained.

While the accuracy rates are shown in Table 2, the percentage values ​​of the incorrect results are shown in Table 3. The answers obtained from each approach are compared with the real answer values. The error percentage scores for GPT-4o vary between 4.6% and 15.1%, and the error percentage scores for Gemini-2.0-flash vary between 11.5% and 25.1%.

GPT-4o with Self-Consistency has the lowest error rate (4.6%). This suggests that internal reasoning is reliable. However, GPT-4o with Zero-Shot has the highest error rate (15.1%). This suggests that reasoning and verification cases are incomplete, leading to interpretive errors. This shows that the answers obtained with Self-Consistency are more consistent and accurate, while Zero-Shot gives more wrong answers. If we examine Gemini-2.0-flash, ToT has the lowest error percentage (11.5%), while Zero-Shot again has the highest error percentage (25.1%). When this situation is examined in terms of both GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash, it is seen that prompt strategies, especially those that include structured thinking, reasoning and reasoning solutions like ToT and Self-Consistency, significantly reduce error rates compared to direct-answer methods like Zero-Shot.

Statistical analysis

In this section, the results obtained are statistically evaluated. For each LLM model, four different prompting techniques (Zero-Shot, CoT, Self-Consistency, and ToT) were run five times. Therefore, a one-way ANOVA was applied to examine the performance differences between the different prompting techniques in the GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash models. The analysis yielded an F-statistic of 323.394 (p < 0.001) for the GPT-4o model and an F-statistic of 429.056 (p < 0.001) for the Gemini-2.0-flash model. These values ​​indicate that the performance differences between the prompting methods used in both models are not random but statistically significant. In other words, there are significant and reliable performance differences between the different prompting techniques.

Table 4 shows the overall comparison performance of the developed system according to its accuracy values. Bootstrap (resampling with 10000 iterations) and Cohen’s h were used to examine confidence intervals and effect sizes in the statistical analysis. This table shows the overall performances, 95% confidence intervals, differences between models, and effect sizes of four different prompt techniques for the GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash LLM models. According to the results, the Self-Consistency method provided the highest accuracy results for GPT-4o. On the other hand, the ToT method showed the best performance for Gemini-2.0-flash. The effect analysis using Cohen’s h showed that the smallest performance difference between the models was observed with the ToT (h = 0.112, negligible effect), while the largest performance difference was observed with the Self-Consistency (h = 0.394, small) prompt method. In the results of the experimental study, which was conducted with five replications, all confidence intervals were narrow (± 0.5% points), indicating high consistency. Furthermore, when the results are examined, the difference between Zero-Shot (lowest) and ToT (highest) is found to be + 13.55 for Gemini-2.0-flash, while the difference between Zero-Shot (lowest) and Self-Consistency (highest) is found to be + 10.44 for GPT-4o. This suggests that Gemini-2.0-flash requires more structured prompt strategies, while GPT-4o demonstrates significant performance even at the basic level with prompt strategies.

The results obtained from five different runs were examined for the prompt methods, and paired t-tests were applied to compare the performance of GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash. The results are detailed in Table 5. Statistical significance was set at α = 0.05. An examination of the table reveals statistically significant performance differences across the prompt methods (p < 0.001). The mean difference shows the difference in the mean number of correct answers between GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash for each prompt method. The results here also demonstrate the significant superiority of the Self-Consistency method over both models in terms of the mean number of correct answers. This supports the interaction between model architectures and reasoning strategies. Self-Consistency also has the highest t-statistic, demonstrating that the performance difference is strong and consistent.

One-way ANOVA was applied to evaluate the effects of different prompt methods on the difficulty level and category-based performance analysis for each model. The results are presented in Table 6. When evaluated in terms of difficulty level, the high F value and p < 0.05 obtained in all methods indicate that the difficulty level has a statistically significant effect on model performance. In both LLM models, it is understood that increasing difficulty level results in more errors. However, the error increase is more controlled in the GPT-4o model. It is also observed that the error rates of the GPT-4o model are lower than those of Gemini-2.0-flash across all prompt methods. Although the number of errors increases with difficulty level in the Self-Consistency and ToT prompt methods, which share the reasoning infrastructure of GPT-4o, the increase in error is more controlled than in the other methods. The analysis results in terms of category-based analysis show that GPT-4o makes fewer errors, especially in the horticulture field (p < 0.05). This demonstrates that GPT-4o demonstrates significant performance differences across different topics. In contrast, a category-based performance analysis for the Gemini-2.0-flash Zero-Shot method yielded p = 0.0832 (p > 0.05). This suggests that Gemini-2.0-flash demonstrates consistent but generally weak performance across different domains. In summary, the GPT-4o model is more consistent, adaptable, and generally stronger, while the Gemini-2.0-flash model exhibits weaker performance, particularly on challenging questions.

When examining the differences between prompt methods in the ANOVA analysis, a post-hoc pairwise comparison analysis was conducted to determine which method pairs accounted for these differences. The results are presented in Table 7. A paired t-test was used for these comparisons. To prevent spurious significance in multiple comparisons, a Bonferroni correction was applied, and the adjusted significance level was set at α = 0.05/6 ≈ 0.0083. The majority of the results were found to have p < 0.0083, indicating that the differences between the prompt methods were statistically significant and significantly affected model performance. Furthermore, the negative values ​​observed in the t-test results indicate that the second-ranked prompt method outperformed the first-ranked prompt method. This demonstrates that reasoning-based prompt methods, in particular, provide a significant superiority in performance.

An independent samples t-test was applied to evaluate the differences in category-based model performance for both LLM models, and the magnitude of the differences was determined using Cohen’s d effect size. The results are presented in Table 8. According to the analysis results, GPT-4o made fewer errors than Gemini-2.0-flash in most prompt methods and category areas. p > 0.05 was obtained for the CoT-Crop Production, ToT-Crop Production, and ToT-Horticulture pairs, and these differences were not statistically significant. Overall, when the t-test results and Cohen’s d values ​​are examined together, GPT-4o demonstrates statistically significant, large effect size, and superior performance compared to Gemini-2.0-flash. These results demonstrate that GPT-4o offers a significant advantage, particularly in reasoning-based prompt methods. However, the equality between GPT-4o ToT-Horticulture and Gemini-2.0-flash ToT-Horticulture is still present. This indicator is a significant indicator that Gemini-2.0-flash can match GPT-4o’s performance in the ToT-Horticulture category. Furthermore, in the performance analysis of the ToT-Crop Production category, Gemini-2.0-flash appears to outperform GPT-4o.

Discussion

This study was created for the purpose of researching the use of LLMs in the field of agriculture. In this context, a question set was designed to help experts in the field of agriculture. An LLMs-based model was proposed to answer the questions in the multiple-choice question pool. 3 different difficulty levels and 3 different question groups were created in the created question set. Within the scope of the study, 8 different approaches were created, 2 of which are LLMs and 4 are prompt models. In this study, GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash were preferred as LLMs, and Zero-Shot, CoT, Self-Consistency, and ToT were preferred as prompt models.

When the results obtained were evaluated, it was observed that GPT-4o was more successful. In particular, it was revealed as a result of calculations that the answers produced with Self-Consistency, which showed 95.3% success, were correct and consistent, while the answers produced with Zero-Shot, which showed 84.8% success, produced more wrong answers. In the Self-Consistency prompt method, the results produced were evaluated with the majority voting method and the most produced option was selected, making it the most successful prompt method for GPT-4o.

When examined in terms of Gemini-2.0-flash, it was observed that the answers produced with ToT were more accurate and consistent, while the answers produced with Zero-Shot produced more incorrect answers. The reason why the results produced in the ToT prompt model were more successful was evaluated as it provided the most appropriate option to be selected by producing intermediate values.

Detailed statistical analysis was also conducted on the results. This analysis evaluated and interpreted the LLM model, prompt method, difficulty levels, and category-based format using bootstrap confidence intervals, paired t-tests, ANOVA, and effect size measures (Cohen’s h and d).

Each of the major LLM used has its own strengths. Gemini-2.0-flash is a version optimized for speed and energy efficiency, but it did not produce as good results as GPT-4o.

When the obtained results were evaluated in terms of prompt methods;

  • It has been clearly seen that not only the selection of the LLM but also the selection of the prompt method is one of the most important factors affecting the success of the system.

  • The fact that the Zero-Shot prompt method produces only output without using intermediate values ​​or, in other words, without directing the model, reveals why this method is less successful.

  • The reasons such as the use of advanced strategies as a structure, directing the model, thinking in multiple steps, and using the logical reasoning technique of the Self-Consistency and ToT prompt methods have made these methods more successful than other methods.

  • The fact that the prompt method used in the phase of creating the answers with LLMs is not only in the form of questions and answers but also preferred over methods with advanced strategies and logical reasoning ability shows that the answers obtained are more accurate and consistent.

In particular, it is emphasized that the AgriQAs system developed in this study can serve as a user-friendly, intelligent decision support and consulting tool that can easily use by agricultural engineers and agricultural technicians. This LLM-based system developed using optimized prompt techniques, (i) facilitates access to reliable agricultural information, (ii) supports information exchange and transfer, and (iii) pioneers precision agriculture practices. These integrations can increase workflow efficiency, reduce time to access information, and thus increase sustainable agricultural practices.

As a result, the use of LLMs in the field of agriculture will pave the way for many studies to be carried out in this field. It is expected that projects developed with LLMs will create an important leap forward, especially with the increase in the digital environment of the data used in the field of agriculture. LLMs help in the establishment of consultancy systems in the field of agriculture, and the interpretation of explanations. As a result of this, among the future studies, regional specific applicability, i.e., region specific studies can be carried out by taking into account the climate conditions and agricultural areas of the region. In this way, while increasing agricultural productivity, sustainability, and smart agricultural applications, more efficient and higher quality products can be produced.

The current AgriQAs system has yielded promising results by providing answers to the questions prepared. However, in the future, such agricultural-based question-and-answer systems should be integrated into various applications, and some key issues related to these issues should be discussed.

However, the LLM infrastructure used in agricultural systems must be carefully selected due to ethical concerns. Because LLMs have a fairly broad training set in their infrastructure, they may exhibit biases, prioritize different crop types in agricultural applications, and mislead farmers. This could lead to economic losses for farmers relying on the recommendations of such AI-based systems. This raises ethical questions about responsibility. From a different perspective, internet access, in particular, is a key area of ​​debate regarding farmers’ digital literacy skills. The use of LLMs as supporting information in agricultural decision-making systems will be particularly advantageous. Evaluating and then implementing AI-generated recommendation systems, particularly those developed by agricultural experts, will be a significant advantage. However, integrating blockchain-based64 data management to ensure data privacy and transparency is another important topic that needs to be evaluated.

Limitations

While our current study provides valuable insights into the use and evaluation of LLM models with different prompting techniques in the agricultural field, we acknowledge some limitations. Importantly, our study focused on the use of LLMs with different prompting techniques in the agricultural field without comparing them with fine-tuning, RAG pipelines, domain-fine-tuned models, or traditional methods. The key reasons for this deliberate choice were to explore the usability of existing LLMs for agricultural practitioners, to determine whether LLM models could serve agricultural professionals, and to test the validity of the results by conducting a detailed analysis using multiple prompting techniques. Because of this focus, this study serves as a foundation for future studies to integrate and utilize LLM applications in the agricultural field.

Additionally, clear guidelines are needed for the implementation of question-and-answer systems developed in agriculture. Consultation with agricultural experts should be mandatory, particularly in critical scenarios such as those requiring physical examination for disease or pest diagnosis, pesticide/chemical application recommendations, and risky decision-making situations with significant economic implications. However, when considering real-world problems from a cost perspective, factors such as the varying costs of API use for each prompt method and the response time of the developed framework are important for applications developed in this manner. Therefore, such systems should be balanced by considering the intended use, the individuals who will use them, prompt strategies, acceptable response times, and cost balancing.

Future work

Future research should improve the use of LLMs in agriculture. LLM models should be provided with a variety of input options, particularly those frequently encountered by agricultural practitioners, such as missing input, noisy data, and incorrectly worded questions. Comprehensive robustness tests should be employed when evaluating model performance using these inputs. However, the performance of LLM models can only be evaluated on region-specific crops and applications. To ensure more effective use of the frameworks developed, particularly with the assistance of agricultural experts, region-specific datasets should be created, and region-specific studies should be conducted.

In future studies, similar question sets can be prepared and applied to people working in different fields such as agricultural engineers, agricultural technicians, agricultural producers who are engaged in the field of agriculture. However, the results obtained can be evaluated not only in the form of true-false, but also by weighting them according to the easy, medium, and difficult question levels in each question group.

In future developments, such question-answer systems could be integrated with various sensor data for use in Internet of Things (IoT)-based applications. This allows for the creation of real-time, interpretable recommendation systems for farmers by combining data from sensors such as soil moisture, temperature, and pH with live data from satellites, weather forecasts, or camera footage for plant monitoring. Such integrations could be used both to identify plant needs and to guide farmers.
