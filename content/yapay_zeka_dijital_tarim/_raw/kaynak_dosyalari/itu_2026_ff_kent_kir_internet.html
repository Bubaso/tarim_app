The urban–rural divide is narrowing but remains wide in regions with low Internet use


Globally, 82 per cent of urban dwellers use the Internet in 2026, compared with 63 per cent of the rural population.

The urban–rural gap, measured as the ratio of the two percentages, narrowed slightly from 1.4 in 2023 to 1.3 in 2026. The gap is smallest in regions with high Internet use, including Europe, the CIS and the Americas, where the ratio is just 1.1. In Africa, the ratio still stands at 2.2, down from 3.1 in 2023.

In high-income and upper-middle-income countries, where 93 and 88 per cent of the population, respectively, use the Internet, the urban-rural gap has almost been bridged, with an average ratio of 1.1. In low-income countries, by contrast, the gap remains wide with only one in six rural dwellers (17 per cent) online, compared with four in ten urban residents (41 per cent). Nevertheless, the gap has narrowed most in relative terms in low-income countries, with the urban-to-rural ratio falling from 3.8 in 2023, to 2.5 in 2026.
