Meet Wilma: artificial intelligence for Per Plant Farming

Sam Watson Jones
July 17, 2020

Want to see Wilma in action? Sign up for a demo

BOOK A DEMO

Our mission is to work with farmers to create a farming system without compromise. Optimal yield, maximum profitability, or a thriving environment – in farming to achieve at least two of these generally results in compromising on the third. Working with farmers, we have created an entirely new model for ecologically harmonious, efficient, and profitable farming. We call this Per Plant Farming. 

 

Per Plant Farming

We believe per plant farming will become the dominant system as farming goes through the fourth agricultural revolution, maximising yields whilst reducing the impact on the environment. Per-plant farming enables farmers to satisfy the competing demands of profit, yield, and the environment - while improving all three. Less inputs, more outputs; less impact, and more in line with nature. 

 

A new way of farming

This is an entirely new way of farming making the data point the individual plant, and each square metre of soil, rather than the field as a whole. Using robotics and artificial intelligence (AI) therefore brings a new level of precision which is completely beyond the hard limitations of tractors, which necessitate a blanket approach. With Per Plant precision, each plant can be farmed individually, with minimal waste.

At the moment, if a farmer or an agronomist sees weeds in a field, they dose the whole field with herbicide. Many areas of the field will not have a weed problem, so huge amounts of herbicide are wasted each year, which is costly to both the farmer and the environment. The other problem is that herbicides are generally not able to distinguish between different types of weeds (other than at a very high level between grass weeds and broad leaved weeds). However, not all weeds are competitive with the crop (at least up to a certain population density) and some weeds have a beneficial ecological impact, such as providing a food source for invertebrates.


greater biodiversity in farming

The Centre for Ecology and Hydrology have been leading exponents of research into the benefits of greater biodiversity in farming systems. Until now, it has been difficult to create biodiversity within a crop - biodiversity of plant species still tends to be something that happens at the edges of fields. Within the crop, we still have a mindset in farming which is that the target is a 100% monoculture of the commercial crop, with no weeds present.


Per Plant Farming has the potential to change that mindset in farming. It will entirely change what is possible on our farms


Introducing Wilma

At the heart of our robotic farming system is our AI-driven Advice Engine Wilma. The brains of the operation, which will get farmers 'robot ready'. Wilma will allow farmers to pull together and aggregate multiple layers of farm data and farm management information. This will give farmers a much more holistic view of their farms, supporting much more informed decision-making. Working together with our Tom, Dick and Harry robots, Wilma will also enable farmers to manage their farms and take remedial action with per plant precision. 

With Wilma, farmers can see exactly how many weeds they have in their fields - Tom will individually count every weed in the field. The farmer can now select the aggressiveness of their weeding programme, determining whether they want to eliminate all or most weeds, or whether they are happy to leave certain weeds in place up to a certain population density.

 

The first milestone: per plant mapping - is now being commercially delivered. Wilma can see every single plant, every weed, every wormhole. Today, we are delivering an AI-generated field map which has the potential to show the exact location of every plant and all of the weeds on UK farms, and we believe we are the first to market with a product of this sort. This year we aim to demonstrate our ability to kill these weeds autonomously without using chemicals.

 

Where we want to take this

Soon farmers will be able to categorise their weeding programme on a per weed basis. For example, they will be able select a zero tolerance for blackgrass (the most problematic grass weed for wheat farmer), but a higher tolerance for field pansy, which is much less competitive with the wheat.

Our AI will pull on the research that has been done around the ecological benefits of different weeds and help the farmer to make a decision that balances commercial performance with biodiversity. We will be able to index the biodiversity of the farm and its ecological performance by measuring factors such as birdlife, beneficial pollinators, and insect populations.

 

Automated soil sampling

Automated soil sampling will give us a granular understanding of the soil, and the variations in soil health in any individual field, as a digital product. There are two opportunities here regarding soil. 

  • First, in the short term, measuring soil quality with a view to claiming Public Goods payments further down the line.

  • Second, longer term and particularly with the Net Zero targets in mind, there is an opportunity to measure carbon capture with a view also to establishing carbon trading markets.

We are looking to build the ultimate Advice Engine for farmers. We will aggregate data from multiple other sources, to build a comprehensive big data crop model for all Wilma’s decision-making. Decision making in farming is extremely complex. Whether we are going to make a profit on the wheat that we are just about to drill is based on numerous factors - seed spacing, seed depth and variety choice based on soil type and soil moisture, operational timeliness throughout the season, disease pressure, pest pressure, weather at the crucial parts of the growing season, storage conditions, global market movements and so on. This is far from a complete list. 

Added to that is the ability to learn from the aggregated network data of other farmers using Wilma. You will not only learn every year from the crops you are managing on your farm, but also from similar crops being managed anywhere else in the world under this new form of digital farm management. This is when disruption really kicks in and change will start happening much more rapidly. The fully-fledged digital farm will comprise a much broader ecosystem encompassing robotics, drones, autonomous vehicles, and artificial intelligence. Fundamentally, we’re looking at the Digital Transformation of Agriculture.

Ultimately, we will be able to employ permaculture techniques at scale - using gardening tactics such as companion planting, but for broadacre crops. Different crops could be planted alongside each other in the same field, and harvested at different times. An end to monocultures.
